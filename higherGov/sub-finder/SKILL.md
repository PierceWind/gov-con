# Sub Finder

Helps a small business win contracts they can't perform alone, by finding local companies that do the work, checking them, and starting the conversation. Same steps every time.

## Step 0: Get the inputs

You need:
- The scope of work (the statement of work or the Contract Breakdown output)
- The place of performance (city/state or base name)
- The set-aside, if any
- The user's company name and how they want to sign emails

If the user has run the Contract Breakdown skill in this chat, use that. Otherwise ask for the solicitation or the scope. Don't guess the scope.

## Step 1: Read the scope of work

In 2 to 4 plain sentences, say what physically has to be done, how often, and any special requirements (licenses, certifications, equipment, security clearances, insurance, bonding).

## Step 2: Figure out what kind of company can do it

Name the type(s) of business that does this work every day (e.g. "commercial HVAC contractor", "janitorial company with floor care", "marine tank cleaning company"). If the job needs more than one trade, list each one separately.

## Step 3: Find the NAICS code

Give the NAICS code(s) that fit each trade, plus the code on the solicitation if it differs. Use the NAICS code as a search clue, not a filter: many good local companies don't list NAICS codes on their websites.

## Step 4: Search near the place of performance

Search the web for each trade near the place of performance. Start within roughly 60 miles and widen only if you find too few.

Useful searches:
- "[trade] near [city, state]"
- "commercial [trade] [city]"
- "[trade] government contractor [state]"
- The SBA's small business search and SAM.gov entity search for registered companies, when available

Only use information you actually found. Take phone numbers, emails and websites from the company's own site. Never invent contact details. If something can't be found, write "not found".

## Step 5: Quick check on each company

For each candidate note:
- Size (looks small / mid / large; employees if stated)
- Relevant past work (commercial or government, similar jobs)
- Certifications or licenses mentioned (state license, 8(a), SDVOSB, WOSB, HUBZone, OSHA, industry certs)
- Distance from the place of performance
- Red flags (closed, residential only, no website, bad reviews about no-shows)

## Step 6: Bring back 2 or 3 real options

Do not dump a list of 50. Give the user the best 2 to 3 per trade, in a table:

| Company | Location / distance | Why they fit | Certs / licenses | Phone | Email | Website |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Then one line on why you ranked #1 first. The user picks; they know relationships and pricing you don't.

If the user asks for a longer list, add the rest below the top picks.

## Step 7: Write the first email

Draft one short outreach email the user can send to each top pick. Rules:
- Subject line that names the job and the location
- 120 words or less
- Plain language, friendly, specific
- Say who the user is, what the government job is (agency, scope, location, timeline), and that they're putting together a bid
- Ask for: interest, a price or rate for the scope, availability, and a capability statement or past work
- Give a reply-by date that leaves time before the bid deadline
- Do not promise the sub the work. Say you're preparing a bid.

Also give a 3-line phone script for a follow-up call.

## Step 8: Hand-off

Remind the user: the skill finds and drafts; the user still makes the calls and locks in a price. Suggest the Proposal and Forms Writer skill once they have a sub and a price.
