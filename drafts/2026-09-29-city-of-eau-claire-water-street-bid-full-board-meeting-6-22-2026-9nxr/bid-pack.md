Summary: The Water Street Business Improvement District (BID) board meeting addresses the planning, regulatory requirements, funding gap, and proposed BID contribution for a major Water Street event intended to showcase corridor businesses through a street closure, live music, art installations, vendors, food, and a beer garden.

Because the City classifies Water Street as a thoroughfare, the event requires enhanced street-closure measures, including jersey barriers or an approved substitute; four buses may be donated for use as street blockers. The City also requires six police officers for four hours each, with final costs dependent on officer assignments, and may charge for towing or resolving vehicles parked before the event.

The organizer successfully avoided an additional no-parking restriction on Chippewa Street. The proposed layout would close portions of Water Street, use a private lot near the mural for the beer garden and stage-related activities, maintain parking in designated lots, and include art activities in the river alleyway and along the street.

The initial BID commitment was $5,000, but the organizer stated that the event is substantially larger than the type of event contemplated when that amount was approved and more comparable to the BID’s fall festival, for which approximately $10,000 is provided.

The preliminary budget includes:

- Three bands
- A stage capable of supporting music in inclement weather
- A professional event-related service
- Art installations
- Five portable toilets and a hand-washing station
- City and police-related expenses
- Permits
- Cleanup
- Staff and contractor time

Anticipated funding includes $4,000 in visitor-grant funds, which are contingent on conducting the event as proposed and must be used for promotion, vendor income, merchandise, food and beverage sales, sponsors, and approximately nine additional $500 micro-sponsorships.

Without additional support, the projected shortfall was approximately $4,000 to $5,000. The organizer cautioned that the event could otherwise lose money and should not establish an unsustainable precedent for future BID programming.

The board approved a motion to allocate an additional $5,000 from Water Street BID financial reserves to ensure the event could proceed, subject to final City Council approval and confirmation of police costs.

The organizer explained that the City had tentatively approved forwarding the street-event proposal to Council, with a vote expected the following day. The separate “Week on Water” passport and sidewalk-activation programming would proceed regardless of the street-event decision.

The event is expected to be promoted heavily after approval, with cleanup and street reopening targeted within approximately one hour after the event, followed by additional cleanup checks.

Board members and Water Street businesses were asked to recruit sponsors, participate in the event, and attend a June 30 business meeting at Cornelius Market from 6:00 to 8:00 p.m. to coordinate sidewalk activations, the passport program, and business outreach.
