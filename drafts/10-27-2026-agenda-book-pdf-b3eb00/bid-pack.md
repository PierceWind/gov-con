Commissioner Hernandez noted that White Sands Boulevard frontage is valuable real estate. He addressed the
many dilapidated homes that are still rebuildable but seemingly forgotten, and urged structuring assistance as loans,
so the city can recoup funds. He also called for zoning amendments requiring multi-sided non-metal facades on
major corridors to prevent metal buildings with short lifespans.
Mayor Pro-Tem Rardin emphasized establishing a consistent, fair plan to treat all properties equally rather than
"picking and choosing", as it has happened in the past. He noted that demolishing large structures, such as the old
Holiday Inn property, could consume up to half the remaining fund balance. City Manager Stockwell noted that many
property owners leave commercial structures vacant for decades without doing any upkeep. He announced that staff
are drafting a vacant building registration ordinance to address this issue, and create financial incentives to develop
or sell.
Mayor McDonald asked if 1st through 10th would be recognized as being more commercialized as opposed to
residential, pointing out that there are people who reside in that area. She also asked how the city will address owners
who are unwilling to sell properties that are not being maintained, and stated the importance of code enforcement
being truly enforceable. City Manager Stockwell said they are working on amendments to the Code for residential
and commercial areas similar to what many cities are already doing countrywide. This would encourage property
owners to recognize their responsibility to the community to either sell or develop the property. The amended codes
will also address the derelict vehicles and junk that often becomes a nuisance in residential neighborhoods. While
he recognized there may be some pushback, he noted it will ultimately come down to the commission, and he
reiterated the importance of standing firm on things that are both legal and ethical.
Mayor Pro-Tem Rardin asked if a decision needed to be made at the meeting, or if the funds could sit in an interest-
yielding account for the time being, and he asked if there is any recourse for the projects that have already been
funded but have failed to take any action. City Manager Stockwell said there is no timeline for when the money needs
to be spent. He reiterated the importance of research when funding projects, and noted that some projects, such as
Sunbaked Biscuits, have defaulted, and it is very unlikely that the funds can be recouped from them.
Mayor McDonald agreed with the recommendations made.
Commissioner Patillo asked if there would be any future LEDA presentations. City Manager Stockwell said no.
Commissioner Rardin stated that if approved, the Holiday Inn project should have stipulations to direct the funds'
focus. City Manager Stockwell stated that the city will recoup funds by selling property to developers. He also
recommended that the city buy properties that are up for sales tax, and getting them into ownership in a way that will
lead towards development.
