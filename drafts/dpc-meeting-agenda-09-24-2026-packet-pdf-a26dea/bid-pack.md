### DPC Meeting Agenda 09.24.2026 - Packet.pdf (posted 2026-09-23)

Summary: The District Plan Commission agenda packet establishes the agenda for the regular meeting scheduled for Thursday, September 24, 2026, at 2:00 p.m., at the Pelican Ponds Conference Room, 9755 Henderson Road, Brighton, Colorado, with remote participation available through Microsoft Teams.

The meeting is to include call to order, roll call, the Pledge of Allegiance, approval of the August 27, 2026, minutes, public comment, staff updates, a Finance and Resource Mobilization subcommittee presentation, review of a draft Community Gardens Recommendation led by Linda Young, upcoming events, announcements, questions, and adjournment.

The packet identifies Andrew Sword as Chair, Liz Palmer as Vice Chair, Linda Young as Secretary, and additional commissioners Daniel Sherman, Cheryn Miller-Crock, Mark Humbert, Terry Beauman, and Bret Baker. The next meeting is scheduled for October 22, 2026.

The included August 27 meeting minutes record approval of the July minutes by a 5-0 vote and summarize ongoing Commission work involving local food security, financing and grants, marketing and agritourism, farm incubator and farmer-pipeline support, community outreach, meeting-format changes, and communications with local farms.

Specific prior discussions included pursuing grants for community garden boxes and school programs, evaluating county-owned farmland and the Mirada property for small-acre farming, exploring an implement library or shed, contacting local farms and food pantries, recruiting commissioners, clarifying quorum and Open Meetings Law procedures, and conducting a future bylaw review.

Staff and commissioners also discussed maintenance and repairs at the Bromley Koizuma-Hishinuma Farm, participation in Brighton-area cultural and agricultural events, and improved outreach materials such as flyers, QR codes, giveaways, identification badges, and volunteer coordination.

The draft Community Gardens Recommendation proposes testing a Brighton “Grow and Give” program that could later be replicated across Adams County.

Under the proposal, residents registering in person for a City of Brighton community garden would receive a bilingual English-Spanish package prioritized around donation and gardening resources, including:

- Current food-pantry and food-assistance contact information
- Donation guidance
- Harvesting and packaging instructions
- Free or low-cost growing classes
- Seeds
- Seed-starting trays
- Sunscreen
- Row markers
- Organic fertilizer
- Kneeling pads
- Gloves
- Trowels

The proposal also recommends:

- Annual laminated garden posters listing organizations that accept fresh produce and their operating hours
- Potential accessibility improvements, such as shade structures, benches, portable restrooms, and other heat-related modifications
- One or more Community Gardener Appreciation events

Potential partners include local food pantries, Brighton Shares The Harvest, Adams County Food Bank and Health Department, Home Depot, Lowe’s, the Greater Brighton Chamber of Commerce, Platte Valley Medical Center, and CSU Extension.

The September 24 discussion is specifically intended to refine the recommendation, determine how information will be maintained and assigned as an ongoing responsibility, establish a standard format and approval process for Commission recommendations, resolve timing before community-garden registration begins in February, and decide how the final recommendation will be presented.

DPCHSV  Community Gardens Recommendations (DRAFT)
Attached is a draft of our first recommendation from the Local Food Security
subcommittee. In the 9/24/26 DPC meeting, in addition to reviewing/modifying the
recommendation itself, we should discuss the following:
1. Anything sustainable needs to be a documented part of somebody’s job. We need to
continue to discuss, agree, and document for future commissioners what our role is
in developing recommendations, but they should not include ongoing tasks that
would be our responsibilities.
2. How do we know how deep to go with our recommendation? This recommendation
could have been as little as “Give the community gardeners a way to know how to
donate their extra produce” or as much as “Here’s exactly what you should include
in the printed information you give them.” I went with what I was comfortable with
and willing to do, with no costs associated with my work.
3. How will we document our recommendations? Should each one have some of the
same criteria? A standard format? What’s our internal process for reviewing and
approving recommendations? We should follow some standards so we know what
to include and what to expect for each one.
4. How/when will we make our recommendations? Group them all together? This one
has a timing issue, as I believe they begin registering community gardeners in
February.
5. Who will present the actual recommendations? Will it be done in a meeting? Sent
via email?

This is a model that could be trialed in Brighton and, if successful, be replicated for other
community gardens in the county.
