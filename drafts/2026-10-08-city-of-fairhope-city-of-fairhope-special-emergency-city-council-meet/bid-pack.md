Summary: The special emergency City Council meeting authorizes and records the City of Fairhope’s response to Hurricane Isaiah, including adoption of Resolution No. 5930-26.

Following Governor Kay Ivey’s statewide emergency declaration effective October 7, 2026, at 4:00 p.m., the Council declared a local state of emergency effective 8:30 a.m. on October 8, 2026, because the developing hurricane could make landfall near Mobile and Baldwin Counties.

The resolution authorizes the mayor and city clerk to execute and attest contracts necessary to address the emergency without public advertisement, pursuant to the Alabama Code. The Council approved the resolution unanimously.

City officials reported that the storm was expected to approach near the Florida-Alabama line as a strong Category 1 or potentially Category 2 hurricane, with anticipated five- to seven-foot storm surge and approximately five to six inches of rainfall.

The City was monitoring impacts to the pier, utilities, roads, trees, and drainage systems; preparing bypass pumps and generators for lift stations; considering shutting off water service in the pier area to prevent system infiltration; evaluating whether to stage mutual-aid electric crews; and planning to keep police dispatch, police, fire, public works, utilities, and customer service operations staffed as conditions allowed.

Crews would not be deployed until conditions were safe. The City planned to communicate through email alerts, social media, customer-service phone lines, and continuing storm updates.

Residents were advised to remain informed through reliable weather sources, charge devices, secure property, prepare for household and pet needs, and avoid driving after the storm except when necessary.

Baldwin County planned to open a pet-friendly shelter at the Baldwin County Fairgrounds in Robertsdale, with transportation available through BRATS.

The City stated that Friday garbage collection was expected to proceed. Residents were directed to secure and promptly retrieve containers, avoid placing storm debris at the curb before the storm, and refrain from post-storm sightseeing or interfering with cleanup operations.

Officials also emphasized generator safety, including never operating generators indoors or in garages, and noted ongoing concerns regarding weakened trees, power outages, medical-device users, and sanitary sewer overflows.
