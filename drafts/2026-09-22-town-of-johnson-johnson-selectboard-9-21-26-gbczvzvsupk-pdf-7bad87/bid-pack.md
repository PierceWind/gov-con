### Description

The selectboard approved adding one portable toilet near the Old Mill Park playground for the remainder of the recreation soccer season. Officials explained that existing toilets near the welcome center do not adequately serve families at the playground, because parents cannot leave other children unattended when one child needs the bathroom. Soccer games and tournaments provide another reason for a toilet closer to the play area and fields.

Staff were asked to identify a suitable location. Officials preferred relocating the existing Legion Field toilet if possible rather than necessarily renting another unit. They directed that the seasonal unit be removed after the recreation season rather than remaining through cold weather.

An existing payment of $355 per month was mentioned, but officials were uncertain how many existing units that amount covered; it is not a confirmed price for the additional toilet. Vendor work could include seasonal rental, relocation, routine servicing, and removal, depending on whether an existing unit is available.

Summary: The town selectboard meeting addressed municipal business, including the status of a proposed FEMA buyout of the Sterling Market property following flood damage, related redevelopment constraints, tax-sale scheduling, election preparations, municipal equipment, and Old Mill Park facilities.

FEMA funding has reportedly been obligated for a buyout rather than flood-proofing because the proposed flood-proofing measures did not satisfy FEMA standards and there were additional state environmental or regulatory challenges. The buyout remains incomplete: the property owner must decide whether to accept it, followed by an independent appraisal, review of replacement-value considerations, brownfield and environmental assessments, a site-reuse plan, and approvals addressing demolition impacts, habitat, riverbanks, and related permitting.

The town clarified that it is not voting on the property disposition at this meeting and may have had authority only at the initial application stage. If accepted, FEMA restrictions would generally prohibit redevelopment and limit future transfer to qualified entities such as another municipality, conservation organization, or nonprofit.

Discussion emphasized the property’s downtown commercial value, existing road, sewer, power, and parking infrastructure, the importance of retaining a grocery store, and possible alternatives involving state assistance, floodplain elevation or mitigation, replacement development, parking, or small retail uses. No alternative was approved.

The board approved the September 8 meeting minutes and an errors-and-omissions certificate adding a parcel owned by Donald Bliss and Diane Ulier. The parcel, created in 2024 and omitted from the village grand list, will have its assessed value changed from $0 to $63,200.

The town also rescheduled its tax sale to December 10 at 10:00 a.m. at the municipal building because additional notices are needed. The sale will involve auctioning delinquent properties; the former owner has a one-year redemption period, and a selectboard member may need to bid for the town if a property receives no public bid. A board member who plans to bid personally cannot serve as the town’s bidder.

Administrative updates included delivery of three free voter booths, exploration of expanded bank-account collateralization with banks and auditors, and possible direct-debit collection of escrow payments through NMRECH. General-election ballots are expected to be mailed by October 1, and the election is scheduled for November 3, with polls open from 7:00 a.m. to 7:00 p.m.

The board may move its November 2 meeting to accommodate election setup. The town is also coordinating microphone upgrades and a standing or mobile public-comment microphone, with a planned dry run for broadcasting an upcoming college meeting through Zoom and YouTube.

Finally, the board considered placing an additional portable toilet near the Old Mill Park playground and soccer fields because the existing units at the Welcome Center are inconvenient for families and tournament users. Estimated rental pricing discussed was approximately $355 per month for the current units, but no final decision is reflected in the provided transcript.
