### Description

Officials discussed future procurement of equipment and services for the 2028 Olympic and Paralympic activation sites, specifically including portable toilets and fencing. In the activation-site report, staff said they had received cost information for 'equipo, como cercos, baños ... portátiles' and were continuing to develop the operating budget and implementation details.

A councilmember specifically raised the cost of securing installations every night and during periods when events are not operating. Staff reported conversations with the Police Department, which wanted assurance that public safety would be controlled, and said there were several ways to manage security with differing costs. No security deployment model or outside security contract was finalized, but the discussion establishes a potential need for site-security services alongside temporary event equipment.

Accessibility was a significant lesson from the previous FIFA events. The Office of Major Events said accessible site design had been lacking at some locations and that outside contractors supplying toilets and similar facilities did not sufficiently understand accessibility requirements. The office intends to focus on this issue for 2028 and asked municipal departments to do the same. Site planning must also address safe entrance and exit, crowd circulation, public transportation access, and public safety.

Officials warned that the City would compete with corporations and other government entities for equipment in 2028, particularly portable toilets, and emphasized early procurement and cost control. The broader activation procurement process is expected to proceed through RFP work in 2027. Final equipment quantities, security requirements, site counts, operating schedules, and contract packaging remain unresolved.

Summary: The special Ad Hoc Committee meeting record addresses Los Angeles’ preparation for the 2028 Olympic and Paralympic Games, including public accountability, human-rights protections, procurement, cultural programming, youth sports, and lessons from FIFA World Cup fan activations.

Public commenters requested a firm deadline—preferably within five months—for submitting and publishing the Olympic plan; monthly public hearings with more than one day’s advance notice; detailed information within two weeks on a proposed $50,000–$100,000 human-rights scholarship program, including funding, eligibility, distribution, timelines, and accounting; financial reports promised under the Games agreement; and measurable human-rights protections, risk-prevention plans, funding metrics, and contractual safeguards addressing workers, people with disabilities, people experiencing homelessness, and other vulnerable Angelenos.

The committee unanimously approved agenda items 3 through 5 concerning:

- Specific expenditure measures.
- A report capturing best practices from FIFA World Cup parties and fan festivals.
- A Cultural Olympiad status report prepared by the Department of Cultural Affairs with the City Administrative Officer, legislative analyst, Mayor’s Office, LA28, and other stakeholders.

The committee also adopted, with an amendment, the fiscal year 2026–27 youth sports plan administered by Los Angeles Parks and Recreation in coordination with LA28.

The plan invests approximately $25 million in youth programming—about $11.7 million less than the prior fiscal year—with no General Fund impact, while continuing no-cost recreational leagues and prioritizing programs sustainable after the Games.

Of the $168 million promised under the broader youth-sports commitment, approximately $107 million had been invested in the prior fiscal year, leaving about $135 million; the department intends to maintain a reserve of under $10 million during the Games.

Programming decisions reduce or eliminate activities requiring specialized third-party vendors, extensive staffing, animals, or costly equipment, while retaining adaptive sports that department staff can operate and expanding internal trainer certification.

Eligibility for scholarships generally includes low-income youth within five miles of qualifying locations, with a stated family-income threshold of approximately $120,000 for a family of three, and waiver options for eligible applicants outside the geographic area.

The amendment directs the department to report on continuing sports grants after LA28 concludes, including potential successor funding entities.

The meeting further reviewed FIFA World Cup “Kicked in the Park” and related fan activations as preparation for the Olympics.

The City reported approximately 45,000 attendees at free, family-oriented park events across Los Angeles and nearly 2,000 survey responses, with 94 percent of respondents reporting satisfaction.

Council members raised concerns about communicating available equipment and training to park directors, ensuring districts affected by Olympic-related service reductions receive adequate support, and making better use of FIFA-purchased screens and other assets.

The committee approved the youth-sports report as amended by a 5–0 vote, while the captions indicate that the meeting continued with the verbal report on World Cup activations and related Olympic planning.
