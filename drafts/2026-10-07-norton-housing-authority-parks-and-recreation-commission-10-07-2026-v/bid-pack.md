Summary: The Parks and Recreation Commission meeting addresses committee administration, finances, and planning for Norton’s upcoming Halloween parade and related community programs. The commission also agreed that recurring agenda items should be removed when no longer relevant.

The parking-lot sponsorship program is continuing, with additional parade sponsors received and a spreadsheet requested for next month showing sponsorship receipts, available balances, and funds previously authorized through town meeting warrant articles. Members discussed increasing sponsorship prices to account for inflation and clarifying the legal rules governing carryover balances in the Parks and Recreation revolving fund, which reportedly contains less than $75,000, including pickleball-related funds and amounts used for lifeguard costs.

The Halloween parade is proceeding with approximately 32 registrations. Organizers will send reminders, registration forms, and payment links to prior participants who have not yet registered. The minimum requested donation increased from $30 to $40.

Norton police and Attleboro police are coordinating parade support, Justin has a follow-up meeting with Norton police, NEMA and several parking areas are confirmed, and organizers are still seeking access to the lot across from the former gym. Potential food vendors and sponsors include Mike and Lenny’s, possibly placing a pizza truck at Tricentennial Park, and Pelagi’s for ice cream.

Organizers are also arranging portable toilets at Tricentennial and the Oasis area, locating the general parade banner, updating the sponsorship banner, reminding St. Mary’s about parade parking and Mass-related access, and confirming participation by returning groups such as Waiton and Reasonable Rentals. The Norton concert/rock band is considering participation, a color guard is confirmed, and the commission is seeking additional fire-department equipment and other entries.

Operational planning focuses on arranging parade staging, separating music-producing entries from groups with amplified speakers, determining the parade order, recruiting day-of volunteers, carrying banners, managing staging lots, and policing areas for trash. Members debated whether police and fire vehicles should use sirens during the parade, noting both the traditional practice and concerns about excessive noise and interference with musical performances.

A motion was taken on asking police and fire departments not to sound sirens while traveling the parade route. The discussion reflects disagreement over whether the request should apply broadly or whether sirens should remain part of the established parade format.

The “Be Good, Do Good” initiative remains on hold for further planning after Halloween. The October costume swap was identified as an earlier activity under that program.
