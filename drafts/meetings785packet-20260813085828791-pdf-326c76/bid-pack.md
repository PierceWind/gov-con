### Description

As part of the city’s review of homelessness sheltering options, staff evaluated a Safe Parking program and outlined potential implementation models and costs for a 6-month pilot. Safe Parking is described as designated, secure parking areas where people living in their vehicles can legally stay overnight, with services such as restrooms, trash disposal, security, and connections to case management and housing resources.

Although staff does not currently recommend Safe Parking as the preferred option, the materials expressly preserve it as an alternative for further exploration in 2027. A contracted operating model is specifically identified. Under Model 2: Contracted, the city would provide 5-10 spaces and issue a Request for Proposal contract with a local non-profit for services. The scope listed includes trash services, hand washing stations, porta potties, intake and background checks, and quarterly reports and data sharing. The estimated cost for this contracted 6-month model is $98,000.

Additional next steps, if directed, include site acquisition and outreach already in progress, engagement with 19 faith-based organizations and 8 private properties, evaluation of site feasibility and community considerations, and later establishment of contracts or partnerships. Because the presentation explicitly mentions an RFP contract and a defined service model with cost, this represents a concrete vendor-facing opportunity if Council elects to pursue the Safe Parking alternative.

C.	Extended Producer Responsibility Implementation: Path Forward for Waste Diversion
and Customer Rebates
Emily Hunt, Interim Director of Infrastructure & Utilities
Thornton will be implementing Extended Producer Responsibility (EPR) for its waste
and recycling services later this year. EPR is a policy framework adopted by the state
in 2022 that shifts the financial and operational responsibility for managing packaging,
paper, and other end-of-life products from municipalities and customers to the
producers who manufacture them. As a recycling service provider, Thornton is eligible
under EPR to be reimbursed for the net costs of recycling services. The presentation
will cover how EPR programs are being implemented, what they mean for the city's
existing waste and recycling services, anticipated effects on customer rates, and the
timeline for compliance. Staff will outline both the opportunities EPR presents for
reducing long-term costs to ratepayers and the near-term considerations Council
should be aware of as the city transitions to this new framework.

SYNOPSIS:
Thornton will be implementing Extended Producer Responsibility (EPR) for its waste and recycling
services later this year. EPR is a policy framework adopted by the state in 2022 that shifts the financial
and operational responsibility for managing packaging, paper, and other end-of-life products from
municipalities and customers to the producers who manufacture them. As a recycling service provider,
Thornton is eligible under EPR to be reimbursed for the net costs of recycling services. The presentation
will cover how EPR programs are being implemented, what they mean for the city's existing waste and
recycling services, anticipated effects on customer rates, and the timeline for compliance. Staff will
outline both the opportunities EPR presents for reducing long-term costs to ratepayers and the near-
term considerations Council should be aware of as the city transitions to this new framework.

BACKGROUND	(ANALYSIS/NEXT STEPS/HISTORY): (includes previous City Council action)
Colorado's EPR program for packaging and paper, established under HB22-1355 (the Producer
Responsibility Program for Statewide Recycling Act), makes Colorado the third US state to adopt EPR
for packaging materials and printed paper. The core goal is to shift the financial burden of recycling
from taxpayers and local governments onto the producers who place packaging into the market, while

COMMUNICATION
PAGE 2
expanding access to recycling to more Colorado households by 2035. The program requires covered
producers to join a Producer Responsibility Organization (PRO), which funds and manages the end-of-
life handling of those materials - including collection, processing, and recycling or composting. The
Circular Action Alliance (CAA) was approved by the Colorado Department of Public Health &
Environment (CDPHE) on May 1, 2023, to serve as the state's PRO. Under Colorado’s EPR law, service
providers including local governments like Thornton, Tribes, for profit and nonprofit recycling collectors,
material recovery facilities and transfer stations can be reimbursed for 100% of the net costs of the
eligible recycling services they provide to Colorado residents. The program also provides funding for
compost facilities to improve recovery of compostable products or to reduce contamination. Thornton
is currently finalizing the service agreement with CAA, which will provide reimbursement for recycling
services to Thornton, which will in turn be passed on to Thornton’s waste and recycling customers.
Future code changes are likely necessary to implement portions of the program.

BACKGROUND	(ANALYSIS/NEXT STEPS/HISTORY): (includes previous City Council action)
Rates
The proposed water rate and base charge increase is 3.6%, effective January 1, 2027. This adjustment
will result in an average residential winter water bill of $42.16, an increase of $1.46 per month, and an
average summer water bill of $101.73, an increase of $3.53 per month.
The proposed sewer rate and base charge increase is 5.6%, effective January 1, 2027. This adjustment
will result in an average residential sewer bill of $25.83, an increase of $1.37 per month.
The proposed stormwater rate increase is 6%, effective January 1, 2027. This adjustment will result in
an average residential stormwater bill of $5.81, an increase of $0.33 per month.

for a 5/8-inch and 3/4-inch meter is 15.4%.

For discussion purposes.

the ARC resulting from the requested COLA.

BACKGROUND	(ANALYSIS/NEXT STEPS/HISTORY): (includes previous City Council action)
The Thornton Firefighters' Pension Plan was established in accordance with Colorado law and is a
closed defined benefit pension plan covering full-time firefighters hired prior to April 8, 1978. The Plan
currently has 18 members, consisting of 15 retirees and three beneficiaries, with no actively contributing
members.
The Plan is administered by a separate Pension Board consisting of the Mayor, Finance Director, one
citizen appointee, and three members elected by the Plan participants. Plan assets are managed by

The determination of the accrued liability requires the use of assumptions regarding future economic and
demographic experience. Risk measures, as illustrated in this report, are intended to aid in the
understanding of the effects of future experience differing from the assumptions used in the course of the
actuarial valuation. Risk measures may also help with illustrating the potential volatility in the accrued
liability that results from the differences between actual experience and the actuarial assumptions.
Future actuarial measurements may differ significantly from the current measurements presented in this
report due to such factors as the following: plan experience differing from that anticipated by the
economic or demographic assumptions; changes in economic or demographic assumptions due to
changing conditions; increases or decreases expected as part of the natural operation of the methodology
used for these measurements (such as the end of an amortization period, or additional cost or
contribution requirements based on the plan’s funded status); and changes in plan provisions or
applicable law. The scope of an actuarial valuation does not include an analysis of the potential range of
such future measurements.
Examples of risk that may reasonably be anticipated to significantly affect the plan’s future financial
condition include:
A: Investment risk – actual investment returns may differ from the expected returns;
B: Asset/Liability mismatch – changes in asset values may not match changes in liabilities, thereby
altering the gap between the accrued liability and assets and consequently altering the funded
status and contribution requirements;
C: Contribution risk – actual contributions may differ from expected future contributions. For
example, actual contributions may not be made in accordance with the plan’s funding policy or
material changes may occur in the anticipated number of covered employees or other relevant
contribution base;
D: Longevity risk – members may live longer or shorter than expected and receive pensions for a
period of time other than assumed;
E: Other demographic risks
The effects of certain trends in experience can generally be anticipated. For example if the investment
return since the most recent actuarial valuation is less (or more) than the assumed rate, the cost of the
plan can be expected to increase (or decrease). Likewise if longevity is improving (or worsening), increases
(or decreases) in cost can be anticipated.

Low-Default-Risk  Obligation Measure
Introduction
In December 2021, the Actuarial Standards Board (ASB) adopted a revision to Actuarial Standard of
Practice (ASOP) No. 4, Measuring Pension Obligations and Determining Pension Plan Costs or
Contributions. The revised ASOP No. 4 requires the calculation and disclosure of a liability referred to by
the ASOP as the “Low-Default-Risk Obligation Measure” (LDROM). The rationale that the ASB cited for the
calculation and disclosure of the LDROM was included in the Transmittal Memorandum of ASOP No. 4 and
is presented below (emphasis added):
“The ASB believes that the calculation and disclosure of this measure provides appropriate, useful
information for the intended user regarding the funded status of a pension plan. The calculation
and disclosure of this additional measure is not intended to suggest that this is the “right”
liability measure for a pension plan. However, the ASB does believe that this additional disclosure
provides a more complete assessment of a plan’s funded status and provides additional
information regarding the security of benefits that members have earned as of the
measurement date.”
Comparing the Accrued Liabilities and the LDROM
One of the fundamental financial objectives of a pension plan is to finance each member’s retirement
benefits over the period from the member’s date of hire until the member’s projected date of retirement
(entry age actuarial cost method) as a level percentage of payroll. To fulfill this objective, the discount
rate that is used to value the accrued liabilities is set equal to the expected return on the diversified
portfolio of assets (referred to sometimes as the investment return assumption). The current investment
return assumption is 4.50%.
The LDROM is meant to approximately represent the lump sum cost to a plan to purchase low-default-risk
fixed income securities whose resulting cash flows essentially replicate in timing and amount the benefits
earned (or the costs accrued) as of the measurement date. The LDROM is very dependent upon market
interest rates at the time of the LDROM measurement. The lower the market interest rates, the higher the
LDROM, and vice versa. As of January 1, 2026, the current investment return assumption of 4.50% could
be considered a reasonable rate for the LDROM. This results in no difference between the valuation
accrued liabilities and the LDROM.

Table 17 - Definition of Terms
1.	Actuarial Cost Method
A method for determining the actuarial present value of future benefits and allocating such value
to time periods in the form of a normal cost and an actuarial accrued liability.
2.	Present Value of Future Benefits
This is computed by projecting the total future benefit cash flow from the Plan, using actuarial
assumptions, and then discounting the cash flow to the valuation date.
3.	Normal Cost
Computed differently under different actuarial cost methods, the normal cost generally
represents the value of the portion of the participant's anticipated retirement, termination,
and/or death and disability benefits accrued during a year. Once all active members are retired,
the Normal Cost is $0.
4.	Actuarial Accrued Liability
Computed differently under different actuarial cost methods. Generally actuarial accrued liability
represents the value of the portion of the participant's anticipated retirement, termination,
and/or death and disability benefits accrued as of the valuation date.
5.	Entry Age Actuarial Cost Method
A method under which a participant's actuarial present value of future benefits is allocated on a
level basis over the earnings of the participant between his/her entry into the Plan and his/her
assumed exit.
6.	Unfunded Actuarial Accrued Liability
The difference between total actuarial present value of future benefits over the sum of the
tangible assets of the Plan and the actuarial present value of the members' future normal costs.
The Plan is underfunded if the difference is positive and overfunded if the difference is negative.
7.	Actuarial Value of Assets
The value of cash, investments, and other property belonging to the Plan, as valued by the actuary
for purposes of the actuarial valuation.

MEMORANDUM
DATE: 31 August 2026
TO:  Mayor Jan Kulmann and Members of the City of Thornton City Council
FROM: John Bales, President and Members of the Thornton Firefighters Old Hire Pension Board
SUBJECT: Consideration of 2027 and 2028 Pension Benefits
The Pension Board appreciates the opportunity to present the following information, background and
recommendation to Council for consideration in the up-coming budget process. We are tendering a
proposal for a cost of living (COLA) adjustment for the 12 remaining old-hire plan members and 4
surviving beneficiaries based on the following historical synopsis:
  In a letter, dated January 20, 1976, from City Manager James Castrodale to Mr. Ken Freiberg (a
founding member, 1st TFD Chief & President of TFD pension board), the following was agreed
upon:
“The staff of the City of Thornton will support a recommendation to the City Council for an
escalation clause in the benefits, effective January 1, 1977, based on the following terms and
conditions.”
a. The active firefighters participating in the pension plan would increase their monthly
contribution from the present 5% rate to 6%.
b. The percentage of escalation provided annually shall be the same percentage as the
cost-of-living adjustment (COLA) provided our non-uniform employees. This annual
cost-of-living is contingent upon City Council approval and is generally based upon the
Denver metropolitan area consumer index as prepared by the business department of
Denver University annually.
c. The City’s fringe benefit consultant will look at the present insurance policies and
make recommendations to us for areas of possible self-insurance or possible
modification of existing policies
It was later determined by the City that this letter/agreement was non-binding and was not
valid. In 1978-1979 old-hire plan members had the opportunity to elect out of the local plan
and enroll in the new FPPA-managed defined benefit plan. Many old-hire plan members opted
to stay in the current plan based on the agreement stated in the Castrodale letter. The opt-out
decision was a one-time permanent selection with no right of rescission at a later date. The
membership mistakenly assumed that this was how COLA’s would be administered.

  The Old-Hire firefighters’ percentage of contribution continued to increase over the years up to
8.6% of their salaries to maintain the COLA benefits.
  Retirees received COLA Biennially (every other year) adjustments of 3% from 1981-1985 and an
annual increase of 3% in 1986. Records indicate a 3% increase only in the years 1988, 1990,
1995, 1999 and 2001. A 3% COLA was granted in 2008. A flat rate increase of $150 per month
was approved in 2020 for 2021. A flat rate increase of $250 per month was approved for 2023
and 2024.
  In 1996, while only participating active members of TFD were allowed to serve on the Old Hire
Pension Board, the Actuarial Reports indicated that the fund was sound and the contributions of
the participating members were reduced from 8.6% down to 5%. Additionally, remaining active
fire personnel eligible for retirement could participate in a Deferred Retirement Option Plan
(DROP) where they no longer contributed to the Old Hire Pension. This was implemented to
somewhat keep pace with the new FPPA-managed defined benefit system. Active participating
members of the department were granted additional benefits for longevity (service over
required 20 years for 50% up to 70% of salary at 30 years) that members potentially had access
to in 1978. However, the five (5) previously retired members with over 20 years of active
service, were not granted the same benefit. In 2017 the Old Hire Pension Board recommended
that 5 remaining retired members with over 20 years of service should be treated equitably.
Upon recommendation by the Board and City staff, City Council granted and financed the
benefits for the 5 members. This adjustment did not address the 20%, sixteen-year gap. DROP
participants were not allowed to participate on the pension board with the policies later
changed to allow those retired members to serve.
  In July 2019, the Old Hire Pension Board recommended that the covered retirees receive an
across-the-board $150 per month increase to their pensions. It was recommended by city staff
that an actuarial study be completed before consideration by city council (actuarial studies are
completed biennially). Therefore, the resulting study results were not received until August
2020 and the increase applied in 2021. In 2022, Council approved COLAs of $250/mo. for 2023
and $250/mo. for 2024 based on the 2022 actuarial study.
  A flat rate increase of $250/mo. was also approved for 2025 and $250/mo. for 2026 after the
actuarial study in July 2024.
Economic Indicators
  Social Security
The Firefighter’s retirement income is dependent on the pension board and City Council. Retired
Firefighters are not eligible to participate in Social Security in the state of Colorado. Unless retirees
accumulated the required number of quarters through non-fire employment, they are not eligible to
receive social security benefits. In comparison, Retirees not qualifying for Social Security are
required to self-pay Medicare premiums.

  Cost of Living
The cost-of-living index has increased over 19% since 2022. The cost-of-living index has continually
increased for years. City of Thornton non-public safety employees have received COLAs every year
for the period 2008-2026. Fire Department CBA employees will receive a COLA averaging 5% for
2026, and 5% for 2027. All Thornton Fire retirees in the “New Hire” statewide FPPA pension plan
received an 8.25% lump sum adjustment and received a 3.66% COLA for 2025. The inequity of the
$250/month adjustment for the “Old Hire” Plan members is obvious.
Other cost of living indicators from the Bureau of Labor Statistics include increased energy costs,
gasoline prices, average housing costs, food costs, new vehicles prices, used cars and trucks prices,
apparel costs and medical care services/prescription drugs as well as health insurance have risen
dramatically. These inflationary pressures are not expected to subside anytime soon, if at all.
Recommendation
An actuarial study was completed in July 2026 with 3 potential COLA scenarios. The Old Hire Pension
Board contemplated the scenarios and is recommending the following increased retirement benefit:
An ad-hoc COLA of $250 per month for all current retirees and beneficiaries effective 1/1/2027 and an
additional ad-hoc COLA of $250 per month for all current retirees and beneficiaries effective 1/1/2028.
This represents an on-going funding increase of $38,586 to the present annual contribution and a one-
time contribution of $930,613 to fully fund the Old-Hire plan. This also represents a reduction in costs
from 2025/2026 of $90,300 due to the decrease in the number of Retirees with only an increase of 1
Beneficiary. The plan currently operates in an unfunded status, but the unfunded liability decreases as the
age of the plan increases.

appealing to the Thornton City Council to meaningfully reflect on this request to support the dedicated
retired members, who gave so much of their time, talents and efforts to help make the Thornton Fire
Department the remarkable and proud organization that has always unselfishly served this community.
Thank you for your consideration of this important decision.
