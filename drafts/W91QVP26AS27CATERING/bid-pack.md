- Dinner: 1700–1830.

The meal quantities are estimated requirements and may be adjusted by the Government within the general scope of the contract. The COR will provide meal-count adjustments in accordance with the contract’s ordering and notice procedures.

The meal quantities, supported populations, and serving windows are derived from the AS27 BLS planning spreadsheet. 

5.1.1 Menu and Nutritional Standards

The Contractor shall provide meals that are wholesome, appealing, nutritionally adequate, and culturally appropriate for the supported populations.

The Contractor shall submit a proposed menu cycle with the Catering Work Plan.

Menus shall include a balanced selection of protein, starch or grain, vegetables, fruit and beverages.

Menus shall identify common allergens and provide a reasonable alternative meal for Government-identified dietary or religious restrictions when requested in advance by the COR.

The Contractor shall not substitute menu items without COR approval, except when an immediate substitution is necessary to prevent a food-safety issue or service interruption.

Food shall be obtained only from approved and sanitary sources.

AR 30-22 requires food-service programs to provide nutritious and wholesome food choices, while TB MED 530 requires food procured in support of military operations to originate from a sanitarily approved source or otherwise qualify for an authorized exemption. 

5.1.2 Food-Safety and Sanitation Requirements

The Contractor shall comply with TB MED 530 food-safety and sanitation provisions throughout receipt, storage, preparation, transport, serving, cleaning, and disposal.

5.1.3 Person in Charge

The Contractor shall designate a qualified Person in Charge (PIC) for all food preparation, transport, and meal-serving periods. The PIC shall have authority to stop production, discard unsafe food, correct sanitation deficiencies, and take immediate action to prevent foodborne illness.

The Contractor shall ensure that the PIC and food employees receive required food-safety and sanitation training. Training records shall be maintained at the food establishment or work site and made available to the Regulatory Authority, KO, or COR upon request. TB MED 530 assigns responsibility for safe food operations to the PIC and requires documentation of food-safety training. 

5.1.4 Employee Health, Hygiene, and Training

The Contractor shall ensure all food employees:

Are trained in personal hygiene, handwashing, cross-contamination prevention, cleaning and sanitizing, allergen awareness, and time/temperature control for safety (TCS) foods.

Comply with applicable health-screening and food-employee reporting requirements.

Do not handle food when ill, experiencing symptoms consistent with a foodborne illness, or otherwise restricted from food handling under applicable law or food-safety guidance.

Wear clean and appropriate clothing and maintain hygienic practices during food preparation, transportation, and service.

5.1.5 Temperature Control

The Contractor shall use calibrated food-temperature measuring devices and maintain temperature records for TCS foods during cooking, hot holding, cold holding, transport, and service.

Cold TCS foods shall be maintained at 41°F (5°C) or below.

Hot TCS foods shall be maintained at 135°F (57°C) or above.

Food shall be cooked, cooled, reheated, and held in accordance with TB MED 530 requirements.

Food that cannot be safely maintained within required time and temperature controls shall be discarded.

The Contractor shall maintain temperature logs for each meal period and provide them to the COR upon request.

TB MED 530 establishes the 41°F maximum cold-holding and 135°F minimum hot-holding thresholds for food safety and requires food-safety training to address cooking, refrigerated storage, hot holding, cooling, and reheating of TCS foods. 

5.1.6 Food Transport and Delivery

When meals are prepared off site, the Contractor shall protect food from contamination and maintain required food temperatures during delivery.

Food shall be transported in clean, covered, and properly maintained delivery vehicles or containers.

Insulated food containers used for TCS foods shall be cleaned and sanitized before use and preheated or prechilled, as appropriate.

Each insulated food container shall be labeled with the food item, fill time, product temperature at filling, and the required consume-or-discard time.

The Contractor shall coordinate meal preparation and delivery to minimize holding time before service.

The Contractor shall not transport food with chemicals, fuel, waste, or other contamination sources.

TB MED 530 requires insulated food containers to be cleaned, sanitized, preheated or prechilled, labeled, and coordinated to reduce holding time before service. 

5.1.7 Water and Ice

The Contractor shall provide potable water and ice from approved sources (Approved Food Water Risk Assessment) for food preparation, beverage service, handwashing, cleaning, and sanitizing.

The water system supporting food operations shall have sufficient capacity for cooking, sanitation, handwashing, and warewashing. TB MED 530 requires water used for food preparation, equipment cleaning, sanitizing, and handwashing to be potable and requires adequate water-system capacity for field food operations. 

5.1.8 Cleaning, Sanitizing, and Waste Management

The Contractor shall:

Clean and sanitize food-contact surfaces, utensils, serving equipment, and food-transport containers before use and as required during operations.

Provide adequate handwashing capability for Contractor food-service personnel.

Provide covered waste receptacles and remove food waste and trash from the serving location after each meal period.

Protect food, equipment, and serving areas from pests, dust, weather, and other contamination sources.

Comply with applicable Cambodia requirements for solid-waste, wastewater, grease, and food-waste disposal.

5.1.9 Inspection and Regulatory Oversight

The Contractor shall allow access to the food operation, food-preparation facility, food-delivery equipment, food-temperature records, sanitation records, and food-source documentation for inspection by the Government, COR, Medical Authority, Regulatory Authority, Army Veterinary Services, or other authorized inspectors.

The Contractor shall:

Complete preoperational requirements before beginning meal service.

Correct critical food-safety deficiencies immediately.

Correct other deficiencies within the time directed by the COR or Regulatory Authority.

Suspend service of any food item or operation determined to present an imminent health hazard until corrective actions are completed and accepted.

Provide copies of host-nation licenses, inspection reports, corrective-action reports, and food-safety documentation to the COR upon request.

TB MED 530 requires plan review, approval, and recurring sanitation inspections for commercial food vendors and temporary food establishments. It also directs that food contracts incorporate the Tri-Service Food Code by reference. 

5.1.10 Quality Control

COR and contractor shall maintain a sign in sheet at the beginning of each serving line 

to account for the headcount of meals served. Upon 24-hour notification, the forecasted headcount could change the total PAX per day. The government will do everything to deter headcount change, but, if this change does occur the contractor will be notified immediately by the COR. 

The Contractor shall conduct quality-control inspections before each meal service. Quality-control checks shall include:

Menu compliance.

Meal quantity.

Food appearance and palatability.

Food temperature.

Sanitary condition of equipment and serving area.

Employee hygiene.

Potable-water and ice availability.

Food protection during transport and service.

Waste removal and cleanup.

The Contractor shall immediately notify the COR of any issue that may delay meals, reduce meal quantity, create a food-safety concern, or prevent compliance with this PWS.

Summary: This Performance Work Statement (PWS) establishes the U.S. Government’s requirement for standalone Angkor Sentinel 27 (AS27) catering services at the Multinational Peacekeeping Force Training Center in Krong Chbar Mon, Cambodia, during 14–26 February 2027.

The contractor must provide safe, timely, nutritionally balanced breakfast, lunch, and dinner services for U.S. and Cambodian participants, including all food, beverages, labor, management, cooking and serving equipment, mobile-kitchen or approved off-site cooking capability, insulated transport containers, delivery vehicles, potable water, ice, sanitation supplies, cleaning, waste removal, permits, licenses, and other resources needed for complete meal operations.

Because no permanent cooking facility is available at the serving location, meals must either be prepared using an approved mobile kitchen or produced at an approved commercial facility and transported in compliance with the PWS, Food and Water Risk Assessment, Army food-service requirements, the Tri-Service Food Code, and applicable Cambodian laws.

The estimated recurring meal requirement includes breakfast and dinner for 92 U.S. personnel, with service scheduled from 0600–0730 and 1700–1830, respectively, and meals for approximately 200 Cambodian personnel.

During the estimated 20–21 February cultural-event period, participating personnel traveling to Angkor Wat/Siem Reap will receive breakfast only on 20 February and dinner only on 21 February, while normal catering for approximately 200 Cambodian personnel will continue during the event.

The contractor must submit a Catering Work Plan within five calendar days after award covering:

- Cooking location
- Menu cycle
- Preparation and transport procedures
- Food-safety controls
- Temperature monitoring
- Water and ice sources
- Sanitation and waste procedures
- Staffing and designated Person in Charge
- Equipment
- Emergency responses for refrigeration loss, delivery delays, contamination, or suspected foodborne illness

The PWS requires operational flexibility because exercise quantities, performance dates, and service levels may change. Offerors must provide individual unit pricing to support additions or reductions within the contract’s scope.

The contractor is responsible for continuous staffing, quality control, prompt notification of performance issues within two hours followed by written notice within one day, compliance with the Government’s QASP, and attendance at required meetings.

PKO may provide grid power and water when available and approved, but the contractor must maintain backup generator capability and an immediately available alternate potable-water source.

Personnel must meet host-nation access requirements, comply with U.S. Army, OSHA, and Cambodian safety and sanitation rules, observe facility security and OPSEC restrictions, and return access passes within three days after contract termination or cancellation.

5.1.1 Menu and Nutritional Standards

The Contractor shall provide meals that are wholesome, appealing, nutritionally adequate, and culturally appropriate for the supported populations.

The Contractor shall submit a proposed menu cycle with the Catering Work Plan.

Menus shall include a balanced selection of protein, starch or grain, vegetables, fruit and beverages.

Menus shall identify common allergens and provide a reasonable alternative meal for Government-identified dietary or religious restrictions when requested in advance by the COR.

The Contractor shall not substitute menu items without COR approval, except when an immediate substitution is necessary to prevent a food-safety issue or service interruption.

Food shall be obtained only from approved and sanitary sources.

AR 30-22 requires food-service programs to provide nutritious and wholesome food choices, while TB MED 530 requires food procured in support of military operations to originate from a sanitarily approved source or otherwise qualify for an authorized exemption. 

5.1.2 Food-Safety and Sanitation Requirements

The Contractor shall comply with TB MED 530 food-safety and sanitation provisions throughout receipt, storage, preparation, transport, serving, cleaning, and disposal.

5.1.3 Person in Charge

The Contractor shall designate a qualified Person in Charge (PIC) for all food preparation, transport, and meal-serving periods. The PIC shall have authority to stop production, discard unsafe food, correct sanitation deficiencies, and take immediate action to prevent foodborne illness.

The Contractor shall ensure that the PIC and food employees receive required food-safety and sanitation training. Training records shall be maintained at the food establishment or work site and made available to the Regulatory Authority, KO, or COR upon request. TB MED 530 assigns responsibility for safe food operations to the PIC and requires documentation of food-safety training. 

5.1.4 Employee Health, Hygiene, and Training

The Contractor shall ensure all food employees:

Are trained in personal hygiene, handwashing, cross-contamination prevention, cleaning and sanitizing, allergen awareness, and time/temperature control for safety (TCS) foods.

Comply with applicable health-screening and food-employee reporting requirements.

Do not handle food when ill, experiencing symptoms consistent with a foodborne illness, or otherwise restricted from food handling under applicable law or food-safety guidance.

Wear clean and appropriate clothing and maintain hygienic practices during food preparation, transportation, and service.

5.1.5 Temperature Control

The Contractor shall use calibrated food-temperature measuring devices and maintain temperature records for TCS foods during cooking, hot holding, cold holding, transport, and service.

Cold TCS foods shall be maintained at 41°F (5°C) or below.

Hot TCS foods shall be maintained at 135°F (57°C) or above.

Food shall be cooked, cooled, reheated, and held in accordance with TB MED 530 requirements.

Food that cannot be safely maintained within required time and temperature controls shall be discarded.

The Contractor shall maintain temperature logs for each meal period and provide them to the COR upon request.

TB MED 530 establishes the 41°F maximum cold-holding and 135°F minimum hot-holding thresholds for food safety and requires food-safety training to address cooking, refrigerated storage, hot holding, cooling, and reheating of TCS foods. 

5.1.6 Food Transport and Delivery

When meals are prepared off site, the Contractor shall protect food from contamination and maintain required food temperatures during delivery.

Food shall be transported in clean, covered, and properly maintained delivery vehicles or containers.

Insulated food containers used for TCS foods shall be cleaned and sanitized before use and preheated or prechilled, as appropriate.

Each insulated food container shall be labeled with the food item, fill time, product temperature at filling, and the required consume-or-discard time.

The Contractor shall coordinate meal preparation and delivery to minimize holding time before service.

The Contractor shall not transport food with chemicals, fuel, waste, or other contamination sources.

TB MED 530 requires insulated food containers to be cleaned, sanitized, preheated or prechilled, labeled, and coordinated to reduce holding time before service. 

5.1.7 Water and Ice

The Contractor shall provide potable water and ice from approved sources (Approved Food Water Risk Assessment) for food preparation, beverage service, handwashing, cleaning, and sanitizing.

Power: PKO grid power (via the existing 250 kVA transformer) may be used for the onsite cooking facility when available and approved by the COR (Power Usage at the PKO for catering are only approved for a minimum of 110 KVA usage). The Contractor shall provide a dedicated backup generator sized to fully support its entire food operation if capacity is greater than the minimum allowable capacity usage. The backup generator equipment and its operation/fuel will be paid only when actually utilized due to loss or unavailability of PKO power.

Water: PKO water may be used when available and confirmed suitable. The Contractor shall maintain an independent FWRA-approved alternate potable water source ready for immediate use; this alternate source is paid only when actually utilized.

Waste: Solid waste, black water, and grey water volumes for the overall site (approximately 350 personnel) are covered under the separate BLS contract. The Catering Contractor shall ensure that all catering waste is disposed appropriately of in the BLS Dumpsters. They shall also ensure that any grey and black water are ready for collection by the BLS Contractor.

5.1.8 Cleaning, Sanitizing, and Waste Management

The Contractor shall:

Clean and sanitize food-contact surfaces, utensils, serving equipment, and food-transport containers before use and as required during operations.

Provide adequate handwashing capability for Contractor food-service personnel.

Provide covered waste receptacles and remove food waste and trash from the serving location after each meal period. Dumpsters and Grey/Black Water Removal are available through the BLS contract. 

Protect food, equipment, and serving areas from pests, dust, weather, and other contamination sources.

Comply with applicable Cambodia requirements for solid-waste, wastewater, grease, and food-waste disposal. Overall site solid waste, black water, and grey water volumes for approximately 350 personnel are covered under the separate BLS contract. 

5.1.9 Inspection and Regulatory Oversight

The Contractor shall allow access to the food operation, food-preparation facility, food-delivery equipment, food-temperature records, sanitation records, and food-source documentation for inspection by the Government, COR, Medical Authority, Regulatory Authority, Army Veterinary Services, or other authorized inspectors.

The Contractor shall:

Complete preoperational requirements before beginning meal service.

Correct critical food-safety deficiencies immediately.

Correct other deficiencies within the time directed by the COR or Regulatory Authority.

Suspend service of any food item or operation determined to present an imminent health hazard until corrective actions are completed and accepted.

Provide copies of host-nation licenses, inspection reports, corrective-action reports, and food-safety documentation to the COR upon request.

TB MED 530 requires plan review, approval, and recurring sanitation inspections for commercial food vendors and temporary food establishments. It also directs that food contracts incorporate the Tri-Service Food Code by reference. 

5.1.10 Quality Control

COR and contractor shall maintain a sign in sheet at the beginning of each serving line 

to account for the headcount of meals served. Upon 24-hour notification, the forecasted headcount could change the total PAX per day. The government will do everything to deter headcount change, but, if this change does occur the contractor will be notified immediately by the COR. 

The Contractor shall conduct quality-control inspections before each meal service. Quality-control checks shall include:

Menu compliance.

Meal quantity.

Food appearance and palatability.

Food temperature.

Sanitary condition of equipment and serving area.

Employee hygiene.

Potable-water and ice availability.

Food protection during transport and service.

Waste removal and cleanup.

The Contractor shall immediately notify the COR of any issue that may delay meals, reduce meal quantity, create a food-safety concern, or prevent compliance with this PWS.
