### Description

Commission members discussed researching alternatives to the town's existing portable restroom service. An official identified a P&P portable restroom on the Town Green and noted that additional units are used for July Fourth. Members also identified units at Browns Court park and behind the church, suggesting that at least three existing units might be considered, while acknowledging that they did not know whether all were managed by the same contracting entity.

Wasted was proposed as a company to investigate, with its website identified as wasted.earth. The official described the company as using environmentally friendly restroom liquid and separating urine for conversion into fertilizer for farmers. Members discussed conventional portable units and the company's semi-permanent options, described as resembling small trailers. They emphasized that further research was necessary to establish the actual differences from P&P's practices, servicing requirements, and costs.

The official offered to determine who manages the town's portable restroom contract, examine its terms, and discuss whether a change would be possible. Members noted that lowest-price considerations might affect procurement. They also discussed whether Conservation Reserve Fund support could offset a modest cost premium if the commission endorsed a more sustainable service. Any recommendation would need to be developed and communicated to Josh and potentially the Selectboard; no replacement decision or award was made.

Further research and a meeting packet were proposed for discussion at the next meeting, in November, or another nearby meeting. Members also suggested investigating whether fertilizer recovered through the service could be used in town gardens or municipal plantings as a demonstration of resource recovery. The potential contracting opportunity is portable restroom rental, servicing, and waste management, subject to existing contract arrangements and town approval.

Summary: The conservation commission meeting discussion addresses a planned request for proposals to identify contractors for invasive-species removal and ecological restoration in Richmond.

The RFP has been revised to state a preference for mechanical removal over herbicide use and, more significantly, to make native-plant restoration and revegetation a recurring project objective rather than focusing solely on invasive-species removal.

Native planting language appears throughout the project description, background, objectives, proposed scope of work, and evaluation criteria, although the preference is not mandatory.

Because Riverside County would issue the solicitation and the commission may later seek funding from the Recycling Fund for an environmental reserve-fund application, counsel recommended obtaining Selectboard approval before release.

The commission planned to place the RFP on the Selectboard’s next meeting agenda. The solicitation had not yet been issued, and implementation may occur during the 2027 season.

The commission also reviewed unresolved stakeholder negotiations concerning the draft memorandum of understanding for trail and conservation planning.

The draft is intended to preserve the currently approved management plan while exploring whether a trail north of the VAST trail could be developed in compliance with environmental buffers and protections.

Proposed conditions reportedly include excluding the trail from the regional trail network and prohibiting additional trails.

Potential participants include town officials, Vermont Land Trust, conservation and trail committees, the Rochester Selectboard, and Rochester Mountain Trails.

The role of Rochester Mountain Trails and its relationship with the town’s official trail committee remain disputed.

No party had formally approved the draft. Public comments indicated substantial opposition or requests for more information, so the commission planned further review before presenting it to town officials.

Additional conservation work included evaluating two alternatives for rerouting a section of the VAST trail through or beside wetlands:

1. Constructing an approximately 600-foot boardwalk, estimated at $10,000–$20,000.
2. Moving the trail 50–100 feet uphill and constructing approximately four or five bridges.

The commission discussed documenting invasive-species treatments through mapping and field-data tools, including ACF maps, Survey123, KML exports, and a potential system showing treatment locations, dates, responsible personnel, and outcomes.

Members also reported small volunteer removal sites, “weed of the month” outreach, and a completed reed-removal project whose results were to be compiled for a future meeting.

The commission also discussed possible training on the Interval R23 mapping system.

Finally, the meeting covered proposed Richmond stormwater and wetland regulations addressing construction-period turbidity, post-construction first-flush water quality, and potentially one inch of runoff from newly created impervious surfaces.

The proposed standard would apply primarily to new structures such as sheds, garages, and accessory buildings and would not affect existing development.
