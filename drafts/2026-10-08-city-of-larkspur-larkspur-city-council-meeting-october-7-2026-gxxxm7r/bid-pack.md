Summary: The regular city council meeting presentation reviewed the acquisition, donation, and endowment of the remaining 161-acre King Mountain Estate parcel to the Marin County Open Space District, completing the King Mountain Open Space Preserve.

The King Mountain Open Space Association described a roughly 40-year community effort to prevent residential and commercial development, beginning after earlier proposals contemplated hundreds of homes and additional grading.

The Shelby Cullom Davis Charitable Fund purchased the property in 2026 and donated it for public open-space ownership, including a $2 million maintenance endowment. The parcel encompasses the summit of King Mountain and the entire existing loop trail.

Following the August transfer, the Open Space District opened gates at Ridgecrest and Citron fire roads, cleared the Citron route to the summit, solicited bids to remove a chain-link fence, and held a September 23 ribbon-cutting ceremony.

The meeting also featured the Marin County Office of Emergency Management’s update on countywide preparedness and emergency planning. The county recently updated its emergency operations plan after approximately 12 years of accumulated lessons learned, bringing it into alignment with current state and federal requirements.

The plan documents how Marin County, its cities, towns, and special districts will coordinate before, during, and after all-hazard emergencies, including fires, floods, cyberattacks, infrastructure outages, and benefit disruptions. The Marin County Board of Supervisors approved the plan in June, and local jurisdictions will update their plans to conform with it.

Supplemental annexes will address:

- Family assistance and reunification
- Public communications
- Alert and warning
- Emergency press conferences

The county also plans to issue a six-page public version, maintain the full plan online, and conduct outreach through libraries and pop-up events.

Emergency management officials reported that the countywide Alert Marin test occurred September 26 and emphasized the goal of having at least one subscriber per household. Larkspur has just under 50% of households with a registered subscriber, substantially above the approximately 5–15% participation typical of nationwide opt-in systems but below the county’s target.

New Alert Marin enrollment categories provide proactive notifications for:

- King tides
- Coastal flooding
- Riverine flooding
- Creek flooding

The county is also targeting residents ages 18–40 through social media, movie-theater advertising, online messaging, and bus-stop outreach. Residents are being directed to the Ready Marin program for preparedness actions at home, work, and while traveling.
