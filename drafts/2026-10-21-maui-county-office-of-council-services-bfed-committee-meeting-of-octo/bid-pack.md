Summary: The County Council BFED Committee meeting recording covers discussion of Resolution 26-153, which establishes a sister-city relationship between Maui County and Laoag City, the capital of Ilocos Norte, Philippines, along with two additional agenda items concerning emergency monetary donations and emergency-fund status and use.

The Council had already adopted the sister-city resolution at its September 29, 2026, reconvened meeting, authorizing the mayor to formalize the relationship; no legislative action was scheduled at this committee meeting. The discussion was intended to clarify the relationship’s purpose, anticipated benefits, proposed exchanges, and next steps.

Public testimony supported the relationship, citing the substantial Ilocano and Filipino-American community on Maui, Laoag’s role as a regional commercial, agricultural, tourism, and transportation hub, and its international airport connection through Manila.

Administration representative Mahina Martin stated that the mayor’s office received the Maui Filipino Community Council’s request in August 2026 and is coordinating with Laoag Mayor Alid to execute a memorandum of agreement, followed by a ceremonial gathering involving the Filipino community.

She explained that Maui County’s sister-city program has existed for 62 years and includes 28 sister cities, including 10 in the Philippines, with program oversight transferred to the Office of Public Affairs in March 2026. The proposed Laoag relationship is expected to build on historic Filipino migration and Ilocano roots in Hawaii while supporting cultural, business, educational, economic, and diplomatic ties.

The administration also identified the relationship as potentially supporting its “Maui Keiki Abroad” initiative and broader economic opportunities.

CJ Ancheta of the Maui Filipino Community Council, a Laoag native and Lahainaluna High School mathematics teacher, presented potential implementation areas:

- Reciprocal civic and trade delegations
- Participation in Laoag’s February Pamulinawen Festival
- Youth exchanges
- Agricultural and climate-resilient crop-management collaboration
- Bilateral business and investment forums involving the Maui Chamber of Commerce and Filipino Chamber of Commerce
- University partnerships with Mariano Marcos State University
- Teacher exchanges
- Medical exchange missions
- Disaster-preparedness cooperation addressing typhoons and other shared hazards

Testifiers described Laoag as the “Sunshine City of the North,” noted its historic Spanish-era landmarks and growing economy, and reported a Laoag population of approximately 112,117.
