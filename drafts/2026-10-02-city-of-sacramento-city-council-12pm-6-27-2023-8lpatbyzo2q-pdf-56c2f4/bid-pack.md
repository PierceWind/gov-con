### Description

Council members discussed a proposed health and safety camp-management protocol alongside changes to encampment-response priorities. The proposal would return with information on what could be accomplished within existing budgets, what would require additional funding and what priorities the council would need to establish.

Council Member Talamontes asked, "How do we have more trash pickups throughout the city so that we can have it clean," and added that the discussion should include "showers" and "bathrooms" to help people while making conditions cleaner and safer. Council Member Valenzuela similarly identified bathroom access, clean drinking water and the placement of portable toilets as immediate mitigation questions while longer-term housing capacity is developed.

The city manager said immediate increases in response would initially rely on departmental overtime, with additional resource requests potentially returning to council. The mayor explicitly supported considering more funding for compliance and cleanup, stating, "If it takes $20 million to clean this city, find $20 million." That statement was a call to develop funding options, not an approved contract budget.

Potential outside-vendor opportunities include expanded trash collection, debris and biohazard removal, portable toilet rental and servicing, mobile showers and drinking-water provision at locations the city approves. Staff reported that trash-removal resources had already increased from $2 million to $4 million in the preceding year; the discussion here concerned additional service expansion rather than merely listing that existing appropriation.

Summary: The City Council meeting transcript records a June 27, 2023 workshop and discussion on Sacramento’s homelessness response, including coordination with Sacramento County, encampment outreach and management, ordinance compliance, public health and safety, shelter availability, and enforcement.

City officials emphasized that Sacramento funds more than 1,100 temporary shelter beds nightly and operates a Department of Community Response (DCR) with more than 20 employees conducting daily outreach. The city and county partnership is intended to expand outreach, behavioral-health services, temporary shelter, affordable housing, and permanent housing while preserving the city’s ability to enforce ordinances.

The discussion distinguished service-oriented efforts from compliance activities involving critical infrastructure, schools, sidewalks, private property, parks, trails, and sensitive habitat, while recognizing legal constraints under *Martin v. Boise* and severe-weather injunctions.

The assistant city manager described a three-part citywide homelessness protocol:

1. **Outreach:** Establishing rapport; enrolling individuals in the Homeless Management Information System and coordinated-access systems; conducting needs and mental-health assessments; and making service and shelter referrals.
2. **Camp management:** Reducing encampment impacts; removing trash and biohazards; limiting camps to essential items; and addressing fire and other safety hazards.
3. **Compliance:** Maintaining sidewalk passage; enforcing the 500-foot critical-infrastructure buffer for K–12 schools; and protecting parks and sensitive habitats.

As of the meeting, Sacramento had identified 27 highest-priority, or Level 1, encampments. DCR’s average time to close a 311 request was approximately nine days. More than 13,000 calls had been handled since the beginning of 2023, and approximately 115 Measure O notice-and-demand forms had been submitted, 34 of which related to already-assessed Level 1 sites.

Reported results included removal of more than 27,000 cubic yards of waste and 24,000 hypodermic needles since January 2023. This work was supported by an increase in annual trash-removal funding from $2 million to $4 million and eight crews operating seven days per week.

DCR and Hope Cooperative staff had engaged approximately 2,300 people seeking services or shelter, referred more than 1,000 people to shelter, and placed an estimated 697 individuals into shelter or respite, although officials characterized the figures as preliminary and conservative.

The six-officer, one-sergeant Police Department Impact Team handled 610 service calls during the first five months of the year. Park rangers responded to more than 250 homelessness-related calls and removed substantial quantities of garbage and needles from multiple parks.

Code enforcement addressed occupied RVs and trailers at more than 30 recurring locations and abated 1,119 abandoned, wrecked, dismantled, or inoperative vehicles from public rights-of-way between January and May 2023, subject to limits during severe-weather activations.
