### Description

The 2026 4th of July Event Recap for the City of San Marino indicates several areas where the City expects to make changes for next year’s Independence Day celebration at Lacy Park and along the community parade route. Staff states that event planning, led by the Recreation Division, requires a large amount of coordination between various City departments, community partners, and vendors, and that the event-day activities included the J.P. Blecksmith Memorial Run, Family Fun Zone, community parade, City Club’s Pavilion, food vendors, and fireworks show. The report also notes that attendance increased to 11,898 in 2026 and that the celebration averages around 10,000 attendees over the past five years, suggesting a substantial annual event with recurring outside vendor needs.

Most importantly for future procurement, the report identifies specific vendor-related shortcomings and planned improvements for next year. Staff wrote that the new restroom company delivered the restrooms late and the units did not match, and explicitly stated: “Next year, staff will utilize a different restroom vendor.” This strongly indicates a likely future purchase of portable restroom rental and related support services for the 2027 event. The report also states that “Securing food vendors was a challenge this year” and that next year staff will begin vendor outreach earlier, reach out to more vendors, and secure commitments farther in advance. That suggests an upcoming need for food vending participation and potentially event vendor coordination services.

In addition, staff identified operational needs that may create opportunities for contracted event support services. The City plans to expand the parade staging area by one block, revise the event layout to create a larger designated dance area in front of the stage, and refine the staging and release process with clearer spacing instructions and additional staff or volunteers to maintain parade flow. While not framed as a formal solicitation yet, these statements indicate prospective needs for event logistics support, temporary event operations services, traffic and crowd management equipment, site layout planning, portable sanitation, and possibly related technology or payment-support infrastructure given the report’s reference to Starlink, tap-to-pay, and Square systems working well.

The document concludes that staff will develop viable solutions and recommendations addressing operational, safety, and liability issues for next year’s event. That forward-looking language, combined with the explicit statement that a different restroom vendor will be used next year, makes this the clearest potential contracting lead in the packet.

Operational Staff Briefings
Camp  Lacy Summer  Camp
Camp Lacy provided a fun and engaging summer experience for children
ages 5-10 at Lacy Park. The program was offered through four two-
week sessions and one one-week session, operating Monday through
Friday from 9 AM to 4 PM.  Campers participated in a variety of
recreational activities, games, crafts, and group activities designed to
encourage active play, creativity, and social interaction. Each week also
included a swimming excursion at the Rosemead Aquatic Center and a
field trip to destinations such as Medieval Times, Sky Zone, and the
Aquarium. Camp  Lacy provided participants with a well-rounded
summer  experience that combined recreation, enrichment, outdoor
play, and weekly excursions in a fun and engaging environment.
Freewheelers Summer  Camp
Freewheelers provided an active and engaging summer experience for
youth in grades 4-8. The program was offered through one-week
sessions, operating Monday through Friday from  1 to  6 PM.
Freewheelers encouraged participants to stay active, socialize, explore
the San Marino community by bicycle, and learn important bike safety
practices. Participants traveled to local destinations such as Lacy Park,
Starbucks, The Cubhouse, Garfield Park, and the San Marino Community
Center. Throughout the week, participants also enjoyed a variety of
games and  recreational activities. Each week included a swimming
excursion to the Rosemead Aquatic Center and a field trip to destinations
such as Raging Waters and Knott’s Berry Farm. The program provided
a unique  combination of biking, bike safety education, games,
recreation, community exploration, swimming, and off-site excursions
throughout the summer.

BACKGROUND
Since 1965, the City of San Marino has celebrated the 4th of July with a
community parade and fireworks show at Lacy Park. Over the years, civic
organizations such as the City Club have played an integral role in keeping
this beloved community tradition alive. Since 2006, the Recreation Division
has led the event-day activities at Lacy Park, including the fireworks show.
This year marked the third consecutive year the Division has managed the
community parade. The San Marino City Club has also hosted The Pavilion
since 2014.
DISCUSSION
5-Year Attendance Estimates
This year’s attendance reflects a 24% increase over the previous year. Staff
believe the increase may be attributed, in part, to the holiday falling on a
Saturday, which made attendance more convenient for the community.
Favorable weather, outreach to prior parade participants, and continued
interest in the parade and fireworks show may have also contributed to the
PAGE 15 of 17

increase. Over the past five years, the 4th of July Independence Day
Celebration has averaged around 10,000 attendees, demonstrating the
community’s continued enjoyment and support of this annual tradition.

Event Overview
Event planning, led by the Recreation Division, takes a large amount of
coordination between various City departments, community partners, and
vendors. The activities for the day ran smoothly and included: the J.P.
Blecksmith Memorial Run, Family Fun Zone, community parade, City Club’s
Pavilion, food vendors, and fireworks show. For the second year in a row,
“Stone Soul” was a hit with the audience as they performed classic soul and
Motown hits.
Evaluation of the New Parade Route
The new parade route, which began at Wembley Road rather than West Haven
Road, shortened the route by approximately one-half mile while maintaining
the familiar route along Monterey Road into Lacy Park. The new route worked
well, and staff received significant positive feedback regarding the shorter
distance. The modification reduced participants’ exposure to the heat, reduced
the impact of street closures on the surrounding neighborhood, and improved
event-day operations. Based on this year’s experience, staff identified the
need to expand the staging area and provide additional staffing to assist with
organizing and managing parade entries.
Evaluation of No Dogs Allowed Policy
Implementation of the new no dogs policy went well and helped reduce the
number of pets within the event area. Several attendees indicated that their
dogs were service animals, which is permitted under the policy and the
Americans with Disabilities Act. Overall, attendees were cooperative, and the
policy contributed to a safer and more enjoyable event environment.
Event Accomplishments  – What Went Well
The event was a success, highlighted by smooth operations and strong inter-
departmental collaboration. Below is a list of accomplishments:
  Clean-up and park tear-down were handled efficiently.
  The all-day closure of Lacy Park on July 3 helped ensure large equipment
could be moved safely.
  Safety remained a priority, with no major incidents reported.
  Water barriers along the parade route provided additional safety for the
parade.

Event Challenges – What Didn’t Go Well
While the event was very successful overall, a few areas emerged where
improvements could be made.
  Next year, the parade staging area should be expanded by one block,
extending from Waverly Road to Wembley Road to provide additional
staging space.
  The new restroom company delivered the restrooms late, and the units
did not match. Next year, staff will utilize a different restroom vendor.
  The space available for dancing in front of the stage was limited. Next
year, staff will revise the event layout to create a larger designated
dance area in front of the stage.
  Gaps between parade participants affected the overall flow of the
parade. Next year, staff will refine the staging and release process,
provide clearer spacing instructions to participants, and assign staff or
volunteers to help maintain a more consistent parade flow.
  Securing food vendors was a challenge this year Next year, staff will
begin vendor outreach earlier, reach out to more vendors, and secure
commitments farther in advance of the event.
These refinements will help build on an already strong event foundation.
Looking Ahead
Today, staff hope to receive feedback on the event from the Recreation
Commission. Subsequently, staff will work to develop viable solutions and
recommendations that address operational, safety, and liability issues for next
year’s event.
