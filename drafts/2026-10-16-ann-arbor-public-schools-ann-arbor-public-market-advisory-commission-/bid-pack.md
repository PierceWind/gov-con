### Description

Staff reported that officials "are still trying to sort out other options for market bathrooms." Additional existing "thrones" were apparently unavailable because they were needed at their current locations. Staff had asked about bringing in portable toilets only on market days but had been told that approach was probably too cost prohibitive.

An alternative suggested to staff was to maintain portable toilets that could be locked. Staff noted that this was the previous arrangement until the locking mechanism was broken off and was not repaired. The need remains unresolved: "hopefully we can figure that out because I know that that's been an ongoing issue for people."

Staff and commissioners also described operating problems with the existing restroom access mechanisms, including units reporting that they were occupied when empty and problems when users entered before the door completed its closing cycle. Staff said technical personnel had suggested there might be a mechanism they could enable to address part of the problem. Separately, management was exploring whether vendors could obtain bathroom access at neighboring Dunbar Tower, potentially through a key card, but no agreement had been reached.

Potential vendor work could involve portable restroom rental, servicing and secure access arrangements. No option, budget or acquisition date was approved.

Summary: The Ann Arbor Public Market Advisory Commission meeting recording covers agenda approval, September meeting-minutes corrections and approval, market programming, staffing, vendor stall assignments, commission membership, and food-assistance program updates; the captions are identified as unofficial YouTube captions rather than official minutes.

Commissioners Flores, Gates, Hardy, and Bunch were present, while Commissioners Nemeth and Brinkerhoff were absent. The commission approved the agenda and September minutes after noting formatting and text-overlap corrections on pages 4–5.

Public comment included a request for the market to be power washed, with the speaker noting that the facility had not received a full cleaning in years.

Market staff reported on recent and upcoming activities, including a second WIC Produce Connections distribution on September 23 with Gates Greenhouses and Kapnick Orchards; an Ann Arbor Symphony Orchestra brass-quintet performance on September 26; the final farmer-resource fair and an A20 Eats guided market-skills tour on October 7; and the final cooking demonstration of the season, funded through a Michigan Farmers Market Association food-safety grant supported by the Michigan Department of Agriculture.

Upcoming events included an October food-truck rally with 12 trucks, carts, and pop-ups; music; children’s activities; a costume contest; a prize wheel; and Parks Activation decorations. This would be followed by a Halloween market featuring candy stations, vendor costumes, decorations, and possible pumpkin or gourd activities.

The Wednesday before Thanksgiving was identified as the primary remaining high-volume seasonal event.

Operational updates noted that Assistant Market Manager and Inspector Tyler was on a two-month leave and expected to return at the end of November, while Jade had returned from leave. Staff from Natural Areas Preservation were being used to cover shortages.

The annual vendor stall move-up occurred September 19, resulting in one artisan and seven producers gaining seniority. The process was described as generally uneventful.

Helen Byrd was formally welcomed as a daily-vendor and artisan representative, and the commission discussed filling the remaining community-member seat.

Food-assistance reporting showed a daily Double Up Food Bucks match of up to $50 per Bridge Card, approximately $35,314 in Double Up benefits and $39,103 in related program spending recorded before the latest market, and $1,550 spent through Prescription for Health.

WIC Produce Connections was scheduled to end November 11, while Senior Project Fresh was scheduled to end October 31. Commissioners raised ongoing concerns about participants’ confusion over differing Senior Project Fresh balances, multiple cards, language barriers, and the lack of on-site staff able to verify balances, particularly on Saturdays.
