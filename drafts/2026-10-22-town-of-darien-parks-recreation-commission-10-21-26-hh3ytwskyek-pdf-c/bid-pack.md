### Description

Staff proposed repurposing at least part of the Pear Tree concession area into one or more family restrooms. Existing ADA renovations left only one toilet in each restroom; when one is unavailable, service is limited, contributing to the need for a year-round portable toilet. The conversion would provide additional "completely ADA accessible, family accessible" facilities and potentially eliminate that portable toilet. Water and other existing infrastructure already serve the concession area.

The preliminary allowance is $75,000, with staff seeking additional pricing. Staff explained that substantial-improvement restrictions limit spending within a three-year period to 50% of the building's value. A roughly $200,000 building valuation would allow about $100,000, so the proposed amount leaves room for other necessary improvements. Timing is uncertain: staff may advance the project in this budget or defer it because of the department's workload.

Revenue assumptions generally reflect modest fee increases, while boat-permit revenue is projected to rise because of additional kayak racks and higher rates. Paddle-court revenue was reduced to align more closely with FY 2026 actual usage, tennis revenue was retained at $3,500 in anticipation of expanded seasonal reservations at Weed Beach and Cherry Lawn, and park-facility revenue covers paddle-hut use, park events, parties, and picnics.

Camp revenue and expenses increase substantially as an accounting and program-expansion matter rather than because of significantly more campers. Proposed funding includes higher-paid, more experienced camp directors, additional staff to reduce participant-to-staff ratios, more trips, buses, supplies, and entertainment.

Administrative expenses include the merged youth-and-recreation staffing structure, routine salary increases, office temporary help, overtime moved from clerical services, and conference and travel funding for the assistant director and director’s school, the NRPA conference, and the Connecticut State conference.

Beach and court costs include paddle-hut attendants, lifeguards, minimum-wage-related pay adjustments, uniforms, and other seasonal staffing. The one-time Mobi-Mat ADA expense at Pear Tree was removed.

Recreation-facility maintenance covers six full-time parks employees, whose compensation remains subject to negotiations because their union contract expires June 30, 2027, as well as weather-dependent overtime, equipment repairs, trucks, and supplies.

Grounds and facility priorities include:

- Expanded dune and playground maintenance at Weed Beach.
- Recurring weeding and root control at Pear Tree.
- Increased tree maintenance because of storm damage, disease, and beech-tree issues.
- A planned Cherry Lawn tree focus for FY 2028.

The commission also discussed consolidating waste-container costs into facility-maintenance materials, replacing damaged containers and lids, keeping an off-season portable toilet at Pear Tree because no building restrooms are available there, and reducing telecommunications costs by placing Pear Tree service and camp cell phones on seasonal hold.

One-time ADA compliance funding was removed from the upcoming budget after planned signage and related work, while invasive-species funding remains embedded in a larger facility-repair-and-maintenance line.

Sponsorships and donations, including event support such as Rings End’s sponsorship of a band, are currently handled through separate or unclear accounts. Staff are working with the finance director to establish a clearer revenue-and-expense structure.

The commission expressed support for the proposed Parks and Recreation fee increases, and no plan to construct padel facilities was identified.
