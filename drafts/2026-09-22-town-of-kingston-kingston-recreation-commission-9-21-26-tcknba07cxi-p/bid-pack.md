Summary: The Kingston Recreation Commission meeting addresses approval of a special permit application for the 2027 Battle of the Badges public-safety softball tournament, scheduled for August 7 and 8, 2027.

The organizer requested a two-day format to accommodate growth from eight teams five years ago to 32 teams in 2026, including anticipated out-of-state participants, while limiting daily field use to approximately 9:00 a.m. through 5:00 or 6:00 p.m.

The event will be held rain-or-shine with no rain date; unused funds would instead be carried forward to the following year. The organizer also requested use of the concession stand and plans to seek authorization for alcohol service involving 42nd Brewery, subject to the appropriate licensing and Select Board approval.

The Commission unanimously approved the August 7–8, 2027 dates and planned to notify KYO/KYSO and coordinate the required licensing process.

The meeting also reports on the Town’s 300th-anniversary programming, including a successful Old Home Day attended by an estimated 500–600 people, with fireworks, police and fire support, one medical incident, and a minor firefighter-gator issue involving a gate.

The final celebration activity is a historic home tour scheduled for Sunday, October 4, from 11:00 a.m. to 4:00 p.m. Participants will purchase a booklet at the Adams Center identifying tour locations, with shuttle buses available to transport visitors between homes.

The tournament organizer stated that proceeds would support Recreation Commission programs through scholarships or financial assistance for children unable to afford activities and would also include a donation toward athletic-field improvements.

KYSO updates included the introduction of a new vice president, plans to revise the athletic-field sponsorship and sign program and obtain updated 2026 contracts, and consideration of moving the annual golf tournament from September to June or August to improve sponsor and raffle-item participation.

KYSO also reported deteriorated flags at the athletic complex, particularly near fields B4–B6. Town staff will identify sizes and locations and coordinate replacement with Facilities.

A continuing irrigation problem was discussed involving recurring yellow “crop circles” on multiple fields, caused by iron and manganese deposits clogging filters, screens, sprinkler heads, and potentially downstream piping.

The Town is operating only one well at a time because of clogging and pressure limitations. It intends to inspect suspected pipe obstructions with a camera after winterization and receives monthly inspections from its irrigation contractor.

Staff also offered to attend a future KYSO meeting to explain the causes, corrective work, watering limitations, and recommended long-term measures.
