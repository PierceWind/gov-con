### Description

City staff reported that the Heritage Square restroom project has reached approximately 90% construction drawings and is nearly ready to be sent out to bid. The project is being managed by Capital Engineering. Staff expects an invitation for bid, described as a hard bid with a bid schedule matching the construction drawings. The lowest responsible bidder would be brought to City Council for contract award, provided the bid is within budget. Staff said they hope to work with the project manager soon on issuing the bid and obtaining a general contractor to perform the work.

The project will combine two existing restroom units off the alley between Uptown Billiards and the Hopi Trust building into one room, increasing the total number of toilets from two to three. The proposed configuration includes one ADA-accessible restroom partition with an adult changing station required by Arizona law, two smaller restroom partitions, one handwashing sink, and a custodial closet. The closet will provide a mop or janitorial sink and storage for cleaning supplies, paper products, and soap. The Downtown Business Alliance clean team currently cleans the restrooms and will continue cleaning the reconstructed facility; staff did not describe a new cleaning procurement.

The restrooms will not be gender specific. An overhead garage-style door will close and secure the facility when it is not open to the public. The design calls for restroom partitions with a nine-inch gap at the bottom and a height of six feet eight inches beneath a nine-foot ceiling. Staff described these features as intended to discourage prolonged occupancy while retaining privacy for restroom use.

Some specifications remain under discussion with the architect, particularly a red-light/green-light occupancy indication system operated by the stall latches. Staff prefers a cost-effective ceiling-mounted indicator rather than expensive European partitions incorporating illuminated strips. The reference to '$500,000 worth of partitions' was an illustration of an unacceptable expense, not the project estimate.

The entire project budget is $250,000 for design and construction, with Capital Engineering project-management costs also drawing on that amount. Some funding has already been consumed by design and related work, so the amount available for the construction contract is less than $250,000. Staff is awaiting actual bids to establish construction costs.

During an estimated four-to-six-month construction period, temporary portable restroom units will be needed at Heritage Square to replace the existing facilities. Their location and arrangement have not yet been determined. This creates a related potential requirement for portable restroom rental and servicing, either within the construction scope or separately. Staff has also discussed a decorative overhead door, wrap, or other artwork with beautification, arts, and sciences partners, but that embellishment is not yet a confirmed procurement scope.

Summary: The Parks and Recreation Commission meeting discussed Flagstaff’s citywide Parks, Recreation, Open Space, and Events (PROS) master plan update, a 10-year planning effort intended to establish a systemwide vision, identify service gaps and community priorities, and provide short-, mid-, and long-term recommendations for projects, programs, funding, operations, maintenance, sustainability, and resilience.

Kimley-Horn consultant Marissa Peligerini reported that the project is in the needs-assessment phase, supported by approximately 40–50 stakeholder and partner groups, focus-group meetings, pop-up events, public workshops, a project website, and interactive tools, including a survey, map-based comments, an ideas forum, and a fictional $100 prioritization exercise.

The first of three in-person public workshops was scheduled for October 22, 2026, from 5:00 to 7:00 p.m. at the Aquaplex. The project team anticipated completing and presenting the final plan for adoption near the end of summer 2027.

Commissioners identified the following potential priorities:

- Maintaining existing parks and infrastructure.
- Increasing PROS staffing and funding.
- Improving park amenities.
- Expanding connected bicycle and pedestrian routes, including the informal FUTS network.
- Paving or otherwise improving muddy and winter-affected trail segments.
- Developing highly requested amenities, such as a splash pad and outdoor ice-skating rink.
- Ensuring equitable access for underserved neighborhoods and residents without reliable transportation.
- Continuing investment in heavily used areas.
- Improving trash-can and waste-collection infrastructure.
- Supporting park maintenance.
- Planning prudent expansion in anticipation of potentially leaner fiscal conditions and changes in Northern Arizona University enrollment.

The discussion also raised the need to assess partnerships, sponsorships, and other non-taxpayer funding sources. Commissioners noted the lack of a dedicated events facility within Flagstaff city limits and emphasized the importance of balancing investments in events, facilities, parks, recreation, and open space.

The meeting also conveyed a separate city-government change under review. At City Council’s direction, the City Clerk’s Office is evaluating Flagstaff’s approximately 27 boards and commissions and will recommend ways to improve their effectiveness, efficiency, and service to the community.

Issues under consideration include:

- Which bodies are required by state statute.
- Whether commissions or working groups could be combined.
- The staff and council time required to support them.
- Recurring quorum difficulties.
- Whether certain temporary efforts could operate as ad hoc citizen project groups rather than formal commissions subject to open-meeting requirements.

Recommendations were expected to be presented to City Council on November 17, 2026, with materials anticipated the week before. Commissioners and the public may attend, watch online, or submit comments in advance.

Two Parks and Recreation Commission vacancies were expected to be filled by Council on October 20, 2026.
