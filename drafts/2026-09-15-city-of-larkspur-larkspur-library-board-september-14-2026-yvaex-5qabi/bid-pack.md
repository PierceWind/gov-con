Summary: The Larkspur Library Board meeting addressed library operations, service demand, staffing, facility use, data collection, parking, and a proposed policy for displaying community information. Board members approved the consent calendar as corrected, with no public comment and no board member reports.

The Library Director reported record circulation of approximately 11,500 items in August 2026, surpassing July’s previous record of approximately 10,500 items, while noting that the reopening of the Corte Madera library could reduce future circulation. Demand for the outdoor hold-pickup locker has exceeded its capacity by an estimated two to three times, prompting consideration of expansion. Members also discussed improving screen visibility in direct sunlight and the existing maintenance agreement.

The library is recruiting two library assistants from more than 200 applicants. Nine candidates completed first-round interviews, five were scheduled for further consideration, and the library expects to fill two full-time positions. Once hired and trained, the additional staff are expected to support expanded evening and Monday hours, potentially by late 2026 or early 2027.

The Director also highlighted an upcoming city climate-action/resilience forum to be offered in person and via Zoom/Marin TV, a Saturday author event featuring Deborah Santana, and a Friends of the Library book sale that generated approximately $4,000 from primarily discarded library materials. A September 25 meeting was planned among the library’s three support organizations—the Common Foundation, Larkspur Library Foundation, and Friends of the Larkspur Library—to coordinate their activities.

The Board continued developing a framework and potential letter documenting library usage and community impact. Members discussed compiling circulation, program attendance, budget, staffing, funding, community-room usage, per-capita spending, and other data for a future report, while acknowledging that the city does not yet have a door counter or reliable occupancy measurement.

Rather than relying solely on visitor counts, members proposed tracking program attendance, instances when Room 2 must be opened because seating is full, narrative “journal” entries describing unusually high usage, and examples of community groups using the library as a gathering space. The discussion noted that story time had previously drawn more than 100 people and had been moved from the children’s room to a larger room for safety.

The Board also discussed parking constraints during large events, including limited on-site spaces, EV vehicles occupying spaces without charging, overflow at Piper Park and the Lucky Shopping Center, and maneuvering difficulties for larger vehicles.

Finally, staff presented a proposed priority policy for the library’s new hallway slat-wall display. The policy is intended to manage limited promotional space and determine which community notices may be posted, consistent with practices at other libraries and the city’s restriction against distributing handouts or giveaways.
