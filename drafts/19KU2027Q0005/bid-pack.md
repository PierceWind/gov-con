Background
 The U.S. Embassy in Kuwait seeks rental, installation, and maintenance of standby generators for U.S. Government residences at SDH. City-grid outages threaten residence operations and occupants’ safety and security.

Work Details
 - Supply, install, commission, operate, maintain, and repair two 500 kVA standby diesel generators, each with an ATS. Include labor, equipment, materials, permits, cabling, and related services. Survey the site, install at a COR-approved location, connect to the residence main isolator, and remove the equipment and restore the site when the rental ends. The ATS must transfer the residence load to generator power during outages and return it to grid power when service is restored.
- Generators must be Caterpillar C15, Cummins, or equal, with a minimum 500 kVA/400 kW standby rating at 0.8 power factor; 415/230 V, three-phase, four-wire, 50 Hz output; water-cooled diesel engine with frequency regulation of ±0.5% or better; and brushless alternator with AVR and voltage regulation of ±1% or better. Enclosures must be weatherproof and sound-attenuated, rated no more than 75 dBA at 7 m. Provide a digital auto-start controller, required safety alarms and shutdowns, generator-rated main breaker, 12/24 V DC electric start, and automatic battery charger. Equipment must be no more than five years old or under 10,000 run hours and in good working order.
- Provide fuel capacity for at least 72 hours of continuous operation at full rated load, based on manufacturer fuel-consumption data (planning estimate: 100–115 L/hour per 500 kVA set). Tanks must include a fuel gauge, controller-linked low-fuel alarm, vent, lockable fill cap, and drain valve. Fuel lines must be diesel-rated, protected, and leak-free. Supply 10,000 L of initial diesel at commencement and up to 5,000 L more during the rental as needed. Monitor fuel and arrange refills to keep tanks at least 75% full. Fuel above 5,000 L requires prior written Contracting Officer authorization.
- Pricing must include one-time commissioning/decommissioning, mobilization/demobilization, and initial fuel charges, plus monthly rent per generator. Price additional fuel per liter for quantities delivered and accepted. Submit firm-fixed prices in KWD; VAT is not applicable.
- For crane services on Embassy property, coordinate with the POSHO Office before rental or mobilization and meet its approval and safety requirements. Provide valid crane inspection and load-test certificates, operator and applicable rigger qualifications, and lift and job-specific safety plans. Use the designated back gate, ground escort, barricades, and traffic and pedestrian controls. Coordinate a site walkthrough and stay within rated limits. Where practicable, schedule lifts over occupied buildings for weekends, holidays, or after hours.
- Performance standard: no more than one customer complaint per month, with no repeat complaints for the same deficiency.

Period of Performance
 One-month base period, tentatively beginning December 20, 2026, with four one-month options (up to five months total). Options depend on funding availability and the Government’s best interest.

Place of Performance
 U.S. Government residence(s) at SDH, Kuwait; U.S. Embassy Kuwait, Masjid Al Aqsa Street, Bayan, for applicable Embassy-site operations.

Bidder Requirements
 The awardee must be a responsible, licensed contractor. For an equal generator, identify the manufacturer, brand, model, and country of manufacture, and provide technical specifications, manufacturer data sheets, and evidence of compliance. The manufacturer must be established, and the equipment must meet applicable international standards, including relevant ISO and IEC standards. For proposed crane work, provide the required inspection certificates, lift and safety plans, and operator and rigging qualifications. Obtain and maintain Defense Base Act insurance for assigned workers subject to DBA through a U.S. Department of Labor-approved provider.

Summary: This **Request for Quotations (RFQ) issued by the U.S. Embassy Kuwait General Services Office/Procurement Office** solicits fixed-price quotations for the rental, installation, commissioning, operation, maintenance, fuel support, and removal of **two 500 kVA standby diesel generators, each with an Automatic Transfer Switch (ATS)**, for a one-month base period beginning approximately December 20, 2026, with four additional one-month option periods subject to funding and the Government’s best interest.

The generators must be Caterpillar C15, Cummins, or equal, and meet or exceed the following requirements:

- Minimum 500 kVA/400 kW standby rating at 0.8 power factor
- 415/230-volt, three-phase, four-wire output
- 50 Hz frequency
- Water-cooled diesel engines
- Brushless alternators with automatic voltage regulation
- Sound-attenuated, weatherproof enclosures limited to 75 dBA at seven meters
- Digital monitoring and automatic-start controls
- Specified alarm and shutdown protections
- Generator-rated output breakers
- 12/24-volt electric starting systems
- Equipment no more than five years old or 10,000 operating hours

Each unit must include a 5,000-liter fuel tank, an ATS rated for at least 800 amps or 125% of full-load current, and the specified 240-square-millimeter power cabling for connection to the residence main isolator.

The solicitation requests pricing in Kuwaiti dinars for commissioning/decommissioning, mobilization/demobilization, an estimated 10,000 liters of diesel, and the one-month rental and maintenance service.

Separate pricing is required for additional fuel supplied and refueling based on actual quantities ordered, delivered, and accepted. Offerors must include all labor, supervision, equipment, materials, transportation, insurance, overhead, profit, and Defense Base Act insurance costs.

VAT must not be included because of the Embassy’s tax exemption.

The base-period rental is CLIN 0004, while CLINs 0005 through 0008 cover four successive one-month options, each requiring the same generator, ATS, fuel-tank, cabling, installation, and maintenance package. Payment will be made in local currency against monthly invoices, and prices will not be adjusted for cost increases or currency fluctuations.

A site visit is scheduled for **November 24, 2026, at 11:00 a.m. Kuwait local time**, and contractors are strongly encouraged to inspect the premises.

Scope of Work: Rental, Installation, and Maintenance of two (2) a 500 kVA Standby Diesel Generator with Automatic Transfer Switch

1. Background

The U.S. Government (USG) residence(s) at SDH draw power from the city grid. Grid outages disrupt operations and residents' safety and security. The USG needs a rented standby diesel generator. When the grid fails, the generator must start automatically and pick up the residence load through an Automatic Transfer Switch (ATS). When grid power returns, the ATS must transfer the load back to the grid.

2. Objective

The Contractor shall furnish, deliver, install, connect, test, commission, operate, maintain, and remove at the end of the contract two (2) 500 kVA diesel generator sets equipped with ATS. The set shall include a standby fuel tank that holds enough fuel for at least 72 hours (3 days) of continuous operation at maximum load, an ATS, and all power and control cabling needed to connect the generator to the residence's main isolator.

Connect the generator output cables to the main isolator through the ATS.

Test and commission the complete system.

Operate, maintain, and repair the equipment for the full rental period.

Disconnect, remove, demobilize all equipment at contract end and restore the site to its original condition.

4.3 Automatic Transfer Switch (ATS)

Rating: 3-pole or 4-pole (to match the site's neutral and earthing system), rated at least [800] A or the generator's full-load current plus 25%, whichever is greater.

Operation sequence:

The ATS monitors city grid voltage and frequency on all phases continuously.

On grid failure or phase loss, the ATS sends a start signal to the generator after an adjustable delay (0–10 seconds).

Once generator voltage and frequency stabilize, the ATS transfers the load to the generator. Total time from outage to transfer shall not exceed [15] seconds.

When grid power returns and stays stable for an adjustable period (default 5 minutes), the ATS transfers the load back to the grid.

The generator then runs unloaded through an adjustable cool-down period (default 5 minutes) and shuts down automatically.

Interlocking: Mechanical and electrical interlocks that prevent the grid and generator from ever connecting to the load at the same time (no backfeed to the city grid).

Controls: Auto/Manual/Off selector, manual override, source-available and source-connected indicators, and an exercise timer for scheduled test runs.

Enclosure: Minimum IP54 if installed indoors, IP65 if outdoors, lockable.

4.4 Cables and Connections

The Contractor shall supply and install copper power cables, sized for the generator's full-load current plus at least 25% margin, with allowance for voltage drop (maximum 3%) over the actual route length.

Cables shall be armored or installed in conduit/cable tray and protected from vehicles, foot traffic, weather, and UV exposure. Cable crossings at walkways or driveways shall use rated cable protectors or buried ducts.

The Contractor shall terminate cables with properly sized lugs, glands, and phase identification.

The Contractor shall connect the generator output cables to the residence's main isolator through the ATS. The Contractor shall coordinate the shutdown of the main supply with the COR and Facility Manager at least [48] hours in advance and keep the outage to a minimum.

The Contractor shall install control wiring between the ATS and the generator controller for the remote start/stop signal.

4.5 Earthing and Bonding

The Contractor shall earth the generator frame, neutral (per the site's earthing system), fuel tank, and ATS enclosure. Earth resistance shall not exceed [5] ohms. The Contractor shall bond the system to the existing building earth where required.

All work shall also comply with Department of State and Bureau of Overseas Buildings Operations (OBO) facility requirements as directed by the COR.

Earth resistance test.

• Date and Location: The proposed date, time, and exact location of the crane operation.

• Load Information: The estimated weight, dimensions, and nature of the load to be lifted or relocated.

• Fall protection and other applicable personal protective equipment (PPE) requirements. 

• Wind speed, weather conditions, and other environmental factors that may affect safe crane operation, including operations on inclined or uneven terrain.

• The proposed crane setup location, travel route, lifting radius, and load-handling area.

Before finalizing the contract or mobilizing the crane, the requesting office and contractor shall conduct a joint site walkthrough, in coordination with the POSHO Office as required. 

The walkthrough shall identify and assess: 

• The designated crane entrance and access route. 

• Crane setup and operating locations. 

• Driveways, turning areas, and available clearances. 

• Existing structures, overhead obstructions, underground utilities, and other potential hazards. 

• Pedestrian and vehicle traffic patterns. 

• The proposed lifting area and load placement location. 

Any identified hazards shall be addressed and appropriate control measures agreed upon before the operation is approved.

Synthetic slings/ropes and alloy steel chains shall be used, as appropriate for the intended lifting application. The contractor shall confirm that all materials and equipment selected are suitable, properly rated, and in good working condition. 

c. Qualified Crane Operators 

Cranes and hoisting equipment shall be operated exclusively by designated, qualified, and competent personnel. 

The contractor shall provide written proof of the crane operator's qualifications and relevant certifications before the operation. 

d. Qualified Rigging Personnel 

All personnel engaged in rigging activities shall be appropriately qualified and competent for their assigned duties. 

At a minimum, rigging personnel shall: 

Be at least 18 years of age. 

Be capable of communicating effectively with the crane operator, lift supervisor, signal person, and other affected personnel at the worksite. 

Understand and follow applicable rigging procedures and safety requirements. 

Wear and properly use all required personal protective equipment (PPE), including appropriate fall protection equipment whenever required by the task and applicable safety standards. 

5. Additional Safety Requirements During Crane Operations 

The following requirements shall be strictly observed whenever the crane is operating within the Embassy compound or on Embassy property: 

a. Lifting Over Occupied Buildings and Structures 

Any lifting operation involving the movement of loads over existing occupied buildings or structures shall, whenever practicable, be scheduled during weekends, holidays, or after normal working hours to minimize the risk to building occupants. 

Exceptions may be considered in emergency situations, subject to prior written approval from the POSHO Office. 

b. Crane Travel Route and Operating Clearance 

The contractor shall ensure that the crane's designated travel route and operating area are clear of structures, vehicles, pedestrians, and other obstructions before and during crane movement. 

Adequate clearance shall be maintained between the moving or rotating components of the crane and hoisting equipment and any fixed objects to prevent employees from being struck, crushed, or otherwise injured. 

A minimum clearance of 24 inches (61 cm) shall be maintained wherever applicable. 

c. Crane Swing Radius and Barricading 

All accessible areas within the swing radius of the crane's rear or rotating superstructure, whether permanently or temporarily mounted, shall be properly barricaded to prevent employees and other personnel from being struck or caught between the crane and fixed objects. 

The contractor shall ensure that barricades remain in place throughout the operation and that unauthorized personnel do not enter restricted areas. 

d. Continuous Safety Monitoring 

The contractor shall maintain appropriate supervision and communication throughout the crane operation. The designated ground escort and other assigned personnel shall ensure that pedestrians and vehicles remain clear of restricted areas and that the established safety controls are maintained at all times.

SECTION 4 - EVALUATION FACTORS

The award will be given to the lowest-priced, technically acceptable, and responsible offeror. Failure to comply with solicitation instructions or submission requirements may result in a quotation being deemed unacceptable and ineligible for award. The Government may reject quotations that are unreasonably high or low.

The Government intends to evaluate an award without discussion but reserves the right to conduct discussions in accordance with FAR 12.203 and 12.204 if determined to be in the Government’s best interest.

Evaluation Process

Quotations will be evaluated in the following order:

Completeness ReviewQuotations will be reviewed to confirm submission of all required information in accordance with Section 3.

Technical Acceptability

Offerors must provide an adequate technical capability statement that their quote is in compliance with the Equipment Specifications and Contractor Responsibilities outlined within the SOW and all associated Attachments and/or Exhibits of the solicitation. The statement should be sufficient to enable the government to make a meaningful evaluation and arrive at a sound determination as to whether the quote will satisfy the requirements of the Government as stated in this request for quote. 

The Government will evaluate the offeror's technical capability Factor Submission to determine if it demonstrates a clear understanding of the requirements outlined in the SOW and submission requirements in 52.212-1 above based on the following rating definitions.

Technical Capability Rating

Rating

Definition

Acceptable 

Proposal clearly meets the minimum requirements of the solicitation 

Unacceptable 

Proposal does not clearly meet the minimum requirements of the solicitation. 

A quote that is determined to be not technically acceptable will not be evaluated further.

Past Performance 

The Government will assess the offeror’s probability of meeting the minimum past performance solicitation requirements. The offeror will be assessed on its record of relevant and recent past performance information that pertains to the products and services outlines in the solicitation requirements. 

The Government reserves the right to obtain information for use in the evaluation of past performance from any and all sources including sources outside of the Government. This past performance information will be used for the evaluation of past performance. 

Past performance will be initially evaluated to determine if it is recent and relevant. The Government will consider recent past performance as having been performed within the last five (5) years preceding the date of issuance of this solicitation. The Government will consider the past performance relevant if it involves rental of emergency diesel generators and Automatic Transfer Switch (ATS) panels. Recent and relevant past performance will then be evaluated to determine how well the offeror performed on prior contracts and given a rating according to the Past Performance Confidence Rating table, below. 

The Government reserves the right to obtain information for use in the evaluation of past performance from any and all sources, including sources outside of the Government. This past performance information will be used for the evaluation of past performance.

Price Evaluation  

The lowest price will be determined by multiplying the offered prices times the estimated quantities in “Prices - Continuation of SF-1449, block 23”, and arriving at a grand total, including all options.  The Government reserves the right to reject proposals that are unreasonably low or high in price. Price will be evaluated for reasonableness in accordance with FAR 12.204(a).

Responsibility Determination  

The Government will determine contractor responsibility by analyzing whether the apparent successful offeror complies with the requirements of FAR 9.104-1, including:

Equipment availability and delivery schedule 

Adequate financial resources or the ability to obtain them;

Ability to comply with the required performance period, taking into consideration all existing commercial and governmental business commitments;

Scope of Work: Rental, Installation, and Maintenance of two (2) a 500 kVA

1. Background
The U.S. Government (USG) residence(s) at SDH draw power from the city grid. Grid outages disrupt
operations and residents' safety and security. The USG needs a rented standby diesel generator. When
the grid fails, the generator must start automatically and pick up the residence load through an
Automatic Transfer Switch (ATS). When grid power returns, the ATS must transfer the load back to
the grid.
2. Objective
The Contractor shall furnish, deliver, install, connect, test, commission, operate, maintain, and remove
at the end of the contract two (2) 500 kVA diesel generator sets equipped with ATS. The set shall
include a standby fuel tank that holds enough fuel for at least 72 hours (3 days) of continuous operation
at maximum load, an ATS, and all power and control cabling needed to connect the generator to the
residence's main isolator.
3. Scope of Work

permits, and consumables needed to:

2. Deliver and place the generator set, fuel tank, and ATS at the location the COR approves.
3. Supply and install all power cables, control wiring, terminations, and cable protection
between the generator, the ATS, and the main isolator.
4. Connect the generator output cables to the main isolator through the ATS.
5. Test and commission the complete system.
6. Operate, maintain, and repair the equipment for the full rental period.
7. Disconnect, remove, demobilize all equipment at contract end and restore the site to its
original condition.

4.3 Automatic Transfer Switch (ATS)
1. Rating: 3-pole or 4-pole (to match the site's neutral and earthing system), rated at least [800]
A or the generator's full-load current plus 25%, whichever is greater.
2. Operation sequence:
o  The ATS monitors city grid voltage and frequency on all phases continuously.
o  On grid failure or phase loss, the ATS sends a start signal to the generator after an
adjustable delay (0–10 seconds).
o  Once generator voltage and frequency stabilize, the ATS transfers the load to the
generator. Total time from outage to transfer shall not exceed [15] seconds.
o  When grid power returns and stays stable for an adjustable period (default 5 minutes),
the ATS transfers the load back to the grid.
o  The generator then runs unloaded through an adjustable cool-down period (default 5
minutes) and shuts down automatically.
3. Interlocking: Mechanical and electrical interlocks that prevent the grid and generator from
ever connecting to the load at the same time (no backfeed to the city grid).
22

4. Controls: Auto/Manual/Off selector, manual override, source-available and source-
connected indicators, and an exercise timer for scheduled test runs.
5. Enclosure: Minimum IP54 if installed indoors, IP65 if outdoors, lockable.
4.4 Cables and Connections
1. The Contractor shall supply and install copper power cables, sized for the generator's full-
load current plus at least 25% margin, with allowance for voltage drop (maximum 3%) over
the actual route length.
2. Cables shall be armored or installed in conduit/cable tray and protected from vehicles, foot
traffic, weather, and UV exposure. Cable crossings at walkways or driveways shall use rated
cable protectors or buried ducts.
3. The Contractor shall terminate cables with properly sized lugs, glands, and phase
identification.
4. The Contractor shall connect the generator output cables to the residence's main
isolator through the ATS. The Contractor shall coordinate the shutdown of the main supply
with the COR and Facility Manager at least [48] hours in advance and keep the outage to a
minimum.
5. The Contractor shall install control wiring between the ATS and the generator controller for
the remote start/stop signal.
4.5 Earthing and Bonding
The Contractor shall earth the generator frame, neutral (per the site's earthing system), fuel tank, and
ATS enclosure. Earth resistance shall not exceed [5] ohms. The Contractor shall bond the system to
the existing building earth where required.

CRANE  OPERATION REQUIREMENTS  AND SAFETY GUIDELINES
All contractors and vendors intending to provide crane services within the U.S. Embassy
compound or on Embassy property in Kuwait must comply with the following minimum
documentation, operational, and safety requirements.
1. Required Documentation
Prior to the commencement of any crane operation, the contractor shall submit the following
documents for review and recordkeeping:
• Valid crane inspection and load testing reports/certificates.
• Proof of qualification and competency of the crane operator.
• Proof of qualification of personnel assigned to rigging activities, as applicable.
• A lift plan and job-specific safety plan outlining the proposed crane operation.
2. Crane Access and Site Safety Requirements
The contractor shall ensure that the following requirements are strictly observed during crane
operations:
• Crane Access: The crane must enter the Embassy compound through the designated
back gate. Entry through the front gate is not permitted.
• Ground Escort: At least one designated escort shall be available on the ground
throughout the crane operation. The escort shall guide the crane along the approved travel
route and alert pedestrians and other personnel to remain clear of the crane's swing radius
and load-handling area.
• Barricades and Warning Devices: The contractor shall provide all necessary barricades,
warning tape, traffic cones, and appropriate signage to restrict unauthorized access to the
crane's swing area, load-handling area, and general work zone.
• Pedestrian and Vehicle Control: The contractor shall ensure that pedestrians and
vehicles are safely diverted away from the crane's operating and lifting areas.
3. POSHO Coordination and Prior Approval
In accordance with Department of State Office of Building Operations (OBO) and Safety, Health,
and Environmental Management (SHEM) guidelines, all crane rentals and operations within the
Embassy compound or on Embassy property must be coordinated with the Post Occupational Safety
and Health Officer (POSHO) Office before the crane is rented or mobilized.
29

The requesting office shall notify the POSHO Office of the proposed crane operation and
provide the following information:
• Purpose and Scope of Work (SOW): A brief description of the work to be performed,
including the reason for requiring a crane.
• Lift Plan: A detailed description of the proposed lifting operation, including the lifting
sequence and equipment to be used.
• Date and Location: The proposed date, time, and exact location of the crane operation.
• Crane Capacity: The required crane capacity and specifications.
• Load Information: The estimated weight, dimensions, and nature of the load to be lifted or
relocated.
3.1 Lift Plan and Safety Requirements
The contractor shall submit a comprehensive lift plan addressing, at a minimum, the following:
• Engineering controls and relevant calculations to demonstrate that the crane and
associated lifting equipment are suitable for the proposed operation.
• A site-specific safety plan identifying potential hazards and corresponding mitigation
measures.
• Traffic management and access control arrangements, including proposed barriers for
vehicles and pedestrians.
• Fall protection and other applicable personal protective equipment (PPE) requirements.
• Wind speed, weather conditions, and other environmental factors that may affect safe
crane operation, including operations on inclined or uneven terrain.
• Measures to protect the contractor's personnel, Embassy employees, visitors, pedestrians,
and other individuals who may be affected by the operation.
• The proposed crane setup location, travel route, lifting radius, and load-handling area.
3.2 Mandatory Site Walkthrough
Before finalizing the contract or mobilizing the crane, the requesting office and contractor shall
conduct a joint site walkthrough, in coordination with the POSHO Office as required.
The walkthrough shall identify and assess:
• The designated crane entrance and access route.
• Crane setup and operating locations.
• Driveways, turning areas, and available clearances.
• Existing structures, overhead obstructions, underground utilities, and other potential
hazards.
• Pedestrian and vehicle traffic patterns.
• The proposed lifting area and load placement location.
Any identified hazards shall be addressed and appropriate control measures agreed upon before
the operation is approved.

The requesting office and Procurement Office shall ensure that all crane-related safety and
operational requirements are clearly communicated to the contractor/vendor and incorporated into

The contractor shall provide the following assurances and documentation:
a. Crane Inspection and Operating Compliance
The contractor shall provide valid crane inspection and load testing reports/certificates.
The contractor must comply with all applicable manufacturer's instructions, operating
procedures, and recommendations governing the operation of the crane and associated equipment,
including the use of attachments.
Under no circumstances shall the crane's rated load capacity or manufacturer-recommended safe
operating limits be exceeded.
b. Rigging Materials and Equipment
All rigging materials and equipment shall meet applicable safety standards and manufacturer
specifications.
Synthetic slings/ropes and alloy steel chains shall be used, as appropriate for the intended lifting
application. The contractor shall confirm that all materials and equipment selected are suitable,
properly rated, and in good working condition.
c. Qualified Crane Operators
Cranes and hoisting equipment shall be operated exclusively by designated, qualified, and
competent personnel.
The contractor shall provide written proof of the crane operator's qualifications and relevant
certifications before the operation.
d. Qualified Rigging Personnel
All personnel engaged in rigging activities shall be appropriately qualified and competent for
their assigned duties.
At a minimum, rigging personnel shall:
• Be at least 18 years of age.
• Be capable of communicating effectively with the crane operator, lift supervisor, signal
person, and other affected personnel at the worksite.
• Understand and follow applicable rigging procedures and safety requirements.
• Wear and properly use all required personal protective equipment (PPE), including
appropriate fall protection equipment whenever required by the task and applicable safety
standards.
5. Additional Safety Requirements During Crane Operations
The following requirements shall be strictly observed whenever the crane is operating within the
Embassy compound or on Embassy property:

a. Lifting Over Occupied Buildings and Structures
Any lifting operation involving the movement of loads over existing occupied buildings or
structures shall, whenever practicable, be scheduled during weekends, holidays, or after normal
working hours to minimize the risk to building occupants.
Exceptions may be considered in emergency situations, subject to prior written approval from the
POSHO Office.
b. Crane Travel Route and Operating Clearance
The contractor shall ensure that the crane's designated travel route and operating area are clear of
structures, vehicles, pedestrians, and other obstructions before and during crane movement.
Adequate clearance shall be maintained between the moving or rotating components of the crane
and hoisting equipment and any fixed objects to prevent employees from being struck, crushed, or
otherwise injured.
A minimum clearance of 24 inches (61 cm) shall be maintained wherever applicable.

All accessible areas within the swing radius of the crane's rear or rotating superstructure, whether
permanently or temporarily mounted, shall be properly barricaded to prevent employees and other
personnel from being struck or caught between the crane and fixed objects.
The contractor shall ensure that barricades remain in place throughout the operation and that
unauthorized personnel do not enter restricted areas.
d. Continuous Safety Monitoring
The contractor shall maintain appropriate supervision and communication throughout the crane
operation. The designated ground escort and other assigned personnel shall ensure that pedestrians
and vehicles remain clear of restricted areas and that the established safety controls are maintained at
all times.
