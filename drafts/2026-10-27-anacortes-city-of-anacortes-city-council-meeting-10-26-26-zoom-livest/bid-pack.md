Summary: The City Council meeting livestream records Anacortes’ October 26, 2026 meeting, including approval of a nine-item consent agenda, committee reports, public announcements, and presentation of the mayor’s preliminary 2027 budget message.

The meeting was attended by all council members except Mr. Young, whose absence was excused. Announcements included a third-quarter Safety Star Award for wastewater treatment plant employee Jim Raines; a vacancy on the Anacortes Housing Authority Board; upcoming library events; and discussions with the Washington State Department of Ecology regarding permit reform, shoreline permits, stormwater assistance, the wastewater plant’s forthcoming nutrient permit, and cleanup sites at the Port’s log pocket and Ecology’s Custom Plywood site.

Committee reports addressed significant operational and financial matters. The Finance Committee reviewed the upcoming budget and revenue and expenditure options.

The Library and Museum Committee reported increased museum visitation despite reduced staffing and discussed a CivicPlus contract modification creating separate library and museum website banners.

The Public Safety Committee reported that three lateral fire/EMS applicants were in training and expected to serve by year-end. The fire department reduced the projected cost of modernizing radios from $240,000 to $22,500 after obtaining software support from the vendor and continued seeking a replacement community paramedic.

The police department is pursuing eligibility for state sales-tax revenue under HB 2015 and expects to begin collecting the tax in April if qualified. The department is undergoing its sixth consecutive accreditation and plans to continue the jointly funded school resource officer program during the 2027–2028 school year.

The mayor’s budget message states that the proposed 2027 budget is balanced after initially projecting a $2.4 million gap, with minimal service reductions, no utility-tax increase, no business-and-occupation tax, and no involuntary layoffs.

The proposal relies on simplified budget structures, efficiencies and expenditure cuts, shifting eligible costs from the general fund to utility funds, sharply reducing outside-consultant spending, and strategic investments intended to increase future economic activity and revenue.

However, the mayor cautioned that the budget is not sustainable because it postpones general-fund contributions to equipment replacement, paving, and other services.

The presentation identified cumulative inflation of approximately 29% from 2020 through 2026, including projected 2027 increases of $1.6 million for wages, 8% for benefits, 45% for fuel, 6.3% for Skagit 911 fees, 15% for electricity, and an overall 13% utility-rate increase.

The city also anticipates approximately $100,000 in outside legal costs related to ongoing nuisance land-use litigation.
