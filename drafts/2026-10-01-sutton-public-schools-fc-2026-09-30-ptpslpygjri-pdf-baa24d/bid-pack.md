Summary: The finance and warrant advisory committee meeting reviews and recommends action on articles for Sutton’s upcoming town meeting, including revised revenue and expenditure appropriations, stabilization-fund transfers, municipal technology and park-support expenses, and disposition of town-owned property.

Article 1 would increase the total raise-and-appropriate budget by $318,334, funded by approximately $218,000 in additional new-growth tax levy, a $13,000 reduction in local receipts, and more than $113,000 in additional state aid following enactment of the state budget.

Corresponding expenditures include $100,000 for Sutton Public Schools, $169,000 for police/public safety, and $47,000 for general government.

Specific adjustments include $12,000 for a municipal employee time-and-attendance system, a reduction of slightly more than $3,600 to correct an executive-assistant wage-budgeting error, and $2,000 for portable restrooms at American Legion Park and Unity Park during the next spring and summer season.

Articles 2 and 3 were discussed at estimated amounts of $250,000 for other-post-employment benefits and $100,000 for the special education stabilization fund, respectively, using anticipated certified free cash and Medicaid receipts.

Officials explained that the special education stabilization balance was approximately $460,000 after a prior $470,000 drawdown and would rise to approximately $560,000 with the proposed $100,000 contribution.

The committee also reviewed Article 8, involving approximately $500,000 for owner’s project management, preliminary engineering, and conceptual-design work related to a potential school HVAC project.

Officials stated that the work would follow public procurement requirements under Chapter 149 and explained that pursuing a broader HVAC replacement could avoid removing and reinstalling recently reinstalled solar panels at the Simmonian Center.

After closing the public hearing, the committee approved Articles 1 through 10 as written, with unanimous or unopposed votes as recorded; one member abstained on Articles 2 and 3 for the same reason reflected in prior minutes.

Article 4 concerned prior-year bills, although officials indicated that no such bills were expected, and Article 5 was identified as an enterprise-fund appropriation supported by retained earnings.

Article 10 addressed conveying town-owned property associated with the former Manchaug Hotel property to another party, with officials emphasizing that the proposal would give or transfer the property rather than take it from a current owner.

The property history included an old fire, unpaid taxes, and tax takings reportedly recorded under an incorrect name before the town’s ownership was confirmed by counsel; the transcript ends during discussion of Article 10.
